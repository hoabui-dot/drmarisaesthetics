What is Rhinoplasty?

Rhinoplasty, commonly known as a nose job, is a surgical procedure designed to alter the shape, size, or proportions of the nose. It is a highly customizable operation that addresses both aesthetic concerns and functional issues, such as breathing difficulties caused by a deviated septum or enlarged turbinates.

Common Concerns Addressed

  • check_circle
    Dorsal Hump: Reduction of a prominent bump on the bridge of the nose.
  • check_circle
    Bulbous Tip: Refinement of a wide, rounded, or ill-defined nasal tip.
  • check_circle
    Asymmetry: Correction of a crooked nose or uneven nostrils.
  • check_circle
    Breathing Issues: Septoplasty and turbinate reduction to improve airflow.

Are You a Good Candidate?

Ideal candidates for rhinoplasty are individuals seeking improvement, not perfection, in the appearance of their nose. You may be a good candidate if:

  • Your facial growth is complete (typically around age 16 for females, 18 for males).
  • You are physically healthy.
  • You do not smoke or are willing to quit prior to surgery.
  • You have realistic goals and a positive outlook.

Understanding Nasal Anatomy

Rhinoplasty is often considered the most complex of facial surgeries due to the intricate interplay of bone, cartilage, and soft tissue. A successful outcome requires a deep understanding of how these structures relate to one another and to the overall facial aesthetic.

Nasal Bone
Alar Cartilage

The Dorsum (Bridge)

Composed of bone in the upper third and cartilage in the middle third. Modifications here often involve reducing a dorsal hump or building up a flat bridge.

The Apex (Tip)

Formed entirely of cartilage (lower lateral cartilages). Tip refinement requires delicate reshaping to achieve symmetry, projection, and rotation.

Surgical Approaches

Dr. Maris employs advanced techniques tailored to each patient's anatomy. The choice between an open or closed approach depends on the complexity of the required alterations.

Open Rhinoplasty

  • check Involves a small, discreet incision across the columella.
  • check Provides unparalleled visibility of the nasal structures.
  • check Ideal for complex cases, significant tip refinement, or revision surgery.

Closed Rhinoplasty

  • check All incisions are concealed entirely inside the nostrils.
  • check Generally results in slightly less initial swelling.
  • check Best suited for straightforward dorsal hump reductions or minor adjustments.

Recovery Timeline

1-3

Days 1-3: Initial Healing

Maximum swelling and bruising peak. You will wear a nasal splint to protect the new structure. Discomfort is managed with prescribed medication. Head elevation is crucial.

7

Day 7: Splint Removal

The external splint and any sutures are removed in the office. You will get your first look at the new contour, though the nose will still be significantly swollen. Most patients return to work or school.

2-4w

Weeks 2-4: Subsiding Swelling

The majority of obvious swelling resolves. You feel comfortable in social settings. Light cardiovascular exercise can gradually resume, avoiding contact sports or heavy lifting.

1y

1 Year: Final Refinement

Micro-swelling, particularly in the nasal tip, finally dissipates. The ultimate refined shape and crispness of the results are fully realized.

Risks & Considerations

Is it right for you?

Rhinoplasty requires emotional maturity and realistic expectations. The goal is improvement, not perfection.

Potential Risks
  • • Infection or bleeding
  • • Asymmetry
  • • Temporary changes in skin sensation
  • • Breathing difficulties (rare)
Prerequisites
  • • Facial growth is complete
  • • Physically healthy
  • • Non-smoker
  • • Positive outlook and specific goals

Frequently Asked Questions

Does rhinoplasty hurt? expand_more

Most patients report discomfort rather than severe pain. This is typically managed well with prescribed pain medication for the first few days, after which over-the-counter pain relievers are usually sufficient.

When can I return to exercise? expand_more

Light walking is encouraged immediately to promote circulation. More strenuous cardiovascular exercise can generally resume at 3-4 weeks. Contact sports should be avoided for at least 6 weeks.

Will my insurance cover rhinoplasty? expand_more

Rhinoplasty performed purely for aesthetic reasons is not covered by insurance. However, if the procedure includes correction of a functional breathing issue, such as a deviated septum, a portion of the surgery may be covered.
