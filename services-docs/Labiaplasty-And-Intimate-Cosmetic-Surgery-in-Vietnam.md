# SECTION 02 — UNDERSTANDING INTIMATE COSMETIC SURGERY

## Component Type

Educational / foundational section

## Heading

Understanding Intimate Cosmetic Surgery

## Content

Female external genital anatomy varies widely between individuals.

Differences in:

- labial length
- colour
- symmetry
- shape

can all occur within normal anatomy.

A visible or asymmetric labia minora does not automatically indicate:

- a medical problem
- abnormal anatomy
- a need for surgery

The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists emphasizes the broad natural variation of female genital anatomy and advises that measurements alone should not determine whether cosmetic genital surgery is appropriate.

## Possible Reasons for Consultation

Some adults may seek assessment because of concerns such as:

- friction during exercise
- pulling or twisting of labial tissue
- discomfort in particular clothing
- discomfort during sexual activity
- changes after childbirth
- significant personal concern about external anatomy

## Different Anatomical Concerns

In other cases, the concern involves perceived vaginal laxity rather than external labial anatomy.

These are different anatomical problems and require different assessments.

## Services in This Category

- Labiaplasty / External Genital Contouring
- Vaginal Tightening / Vaginoplasty

## Core Message

These procedures should not be presented as interchangeable.

# SECTION 03 — NATURAL VARIATION OF FEMALE GENITAL ANATOMY

## Component Type

Education / expectation-setting / ethical positioning section

## Heading

Natural Variation Is Normal

## Content

There is no single external genital shape, size or degree of symmetry that defines normal anatomy.

Natural variation can include differences in:

- labial length
- labial width
- colour
- contour
- symmetry
- visibility of the labia minora

## Important Clinical Principle

Anatomical difference alone is not a diagnosis.

A consultation should not begin with the assumption:

> "Your anatomy is abnormal."

Instead, it should ask:

- What is concerning you?
- Is there persistent physical discomfort?
- Has anatomy changed?
- Are there functional symptoms?
- What are you hoping surgery will change?
- Is surgery medically and anatomically appropriate?

## Core Message

The purpose of consultation is to understand the patient's concern — not to impose a standardized appearance.

# SECTION 04 — LABIAPLASTY: EXTERNAL GENITAL CONTOURING

## Component Type

Primary procedure detail section

## Heading

Labiaplasty — External Genital Contouring

## Content

Labiaplasty is surgery involving the labial tissue, most commonly the labia minora.

The American Society of Plastic Surgeons describes labiaplasty as surgery that reduces the length or proportions of the labia minora.

## Reasons Some Patients Seek Assessment

- pulling
- twisting
- irritation
- discomfort during cycling
- discomfort during exercise
- discomfort during intercourse
- asymmetry
- personal concern regarding appearance

## Consultation Focus

The purpose of consultation is not to create one standardized genital appearance.

Dr. Maris evaluates the patient's individual anatomy and asks:

- What specific concern is causing you to consider surgery?
- Is there physical discomfort?
- Is one side significantly different from the other?
- Has the anatomy changed following childbirth or another event?
- Have you undergone previous surgery?
- What result are you expecting?
- Is surgery appropriate for the concern being described?

## Core Message

Substantial anatomical variation is normal and should be discussed during consultation.

An adult may request a labiaplasty assessment for functional or cosmetic reasons.

Some patients experience irritation when labial tissue rubs against:

Activities such as:

Labial tissue may occasionally be associated with pulling or discomfort during sexual activity.

Pain during intercourse can have many causes.

It should not automatically be attributed to the labia.

---

One side of the labia can naturally be longer than the other.

Asymmetry itself is common.

Surgery should only be considered after the patient:

- understands normal variation
- understands the limitations of surgery
- still wishes to discuss alteration after informed assessment

---

## Changes After Childbirth

Pregnancy and childbirth can alter:

- genital tissue
- perineal tissue
- tissue elasticity
- perceived anatomy

The exact concern should be identified before determining whether:

- labiaplasty
- another procedure
- medical evaluation
- no cosmetic surgery

is appropriate.

# SECTION 06 — WHAT LABIAPLASTY DOES — AND DOES NOT DO

## Component Type

Expectation setting / evidence limitation section

## Heading

What Labiaplasty Does — and Does Not Do

## Primary Purpose

Labiaplasty primarily changes external labial tissue.

## Labiaplasty Should Not Be Marketed as Guaranteed to

- improve sexual pleasure
- increase orgasm
- prevent infection
- correct urinary problems
- make genital anatomy medically "normal"
- produce perfect symmetry

## Evidence Limitation

Evidence supporting claims that elective cosmetic genital surgery consistently improves sexual function remains limited.

ACOG advises that high-quality evidence on the effectiveness of elective female genital cosmetic surgery is limited.

Counseling should therefore address:

- uncertainty
- potential benefits
- potential limitations
- potential complications

## Core Marketing Rule

DR. MARIS content should focus on:

- anatomy
- physical concerns
- informed choice
- realistic expectations

rather than promising improved sexuality.

# SECTION 07 — VAGINAL TIGHTENING — VAGINOPLASTY

## Component Type

Primary procedure detail section

## Heading

Vaginal Tightening — Vaginoplasty

## Content

Vaginal tightening surgery, also called vaginoplasty in the DR. MARIS price list, addresses the vaginal canal rather than primarily reshaping the external labia.

The American Society of Plastic Surgeons describes aesthetic vaginoplasty as a surgical procedure intended to reduce vaginal laxity by addressing vaginal tissue and, depending on technique, deeper supporting tissues.

## Reasons for Consultation

Some adults seek consultation after:

- childbirth
- perceived changes in vaginal tightness

## Important Clinical Principle

The phrase "vaginal looseness" can describe several different situations.

Symptoms can potentially relate to:

- vaginal tissue changes
- pelvic-floor weakness
- childbirth injury
- pelvic organ prolapse
- urinary symptoms
- pain
- other gynecological conditions
- other pelvic-floor conditions

## Important Rule

Cosmetic surgery should not automatically be assumed to be the correct solution.

If symptoms suggest:

- pelvic-floor dysfunction
- prolapse
- continence problems
- gynecological disease

additional specialist evaluation may be necessary.

# CORE PAGE MESSAGE

Female genital anatomy varies widely between individuals, and anatomical difference alone does not mean surgery is necessary.

Labiaplasty primarily changes external labial tissue.

Vaginal tightening / vaginoplasty addresses different anatomy involving the vaginal canal and related tissues.

These procedures should not be treated as interchangeable.

Symptoms such as pain, urinary problems, pelvic-floor weakness or perceived vaginal laxity may have causes that require additional medical assessment before cosmetic surgery is considered.

Dr. Maris personally discusses the patient's concerns, evaluates anatomy where medically appropriate, develops the surgical plan, performs the surgery and participates in postoperative follow-up.

The objective is not to create one standardized intimate appearance.

The objective is to understand the patient's concern, determine whether surgery can reasonably address it, explain limitations and risks, and support an informed decision.
