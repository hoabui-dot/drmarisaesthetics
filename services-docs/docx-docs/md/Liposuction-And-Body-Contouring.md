Understanding Liposuction and Body Contouring
Liposuction is a surgical procedure used to remove localized fat deposits and reshape specific areas of the body. Common areas include the abdomen, waist, back, upper arms, thighs and chin.
It should not be considered a substitute for weight loss. Mayo Clinic and the American Society of Plastic Surgeons describe liposuction primarily as a body-contouring procedure for localized fat rather than an obesity or general weight-loss treatment.
An important part of planning is determining what is creating the contour concern.
For some patients, the main issue is excess fat with relatively good skin elasticity. Liposuction alone may therefore be considered.
For others, significant skin laxity, tissue redundancy or abdominal wall changes mean that removing fat alone may not provide the desired contour. A tummy tuck, arm lift or thigh lift may need to be considered instead of—or in selected cases together with—liposuction.
This distinction is made after clinical assessment.

360° Liposuction or Tummy Tuck?
These procedures address different anatomical concerns.
Liposuction primarily addresses localized fat.
Abdominoplasty primarily addresses excess abdominal skin and may include repair or tightening of weakened abdominal musculature when clinically appropriate.
The American Society of Plastic Surgeons describes abdominoplasty as a procedure that removes excess skin and fat while restoring weakened abdominal muscles.
If significant loose skin is present, increasing the amount of liposuction does not necessarily solve the underlying skin problem.
For some patients, Dr. Maris may therefore discuss 360° abdomen liposuction together with abdominoplasty and muscle repair. Whether these procedures should be combined depends on anatomy, health, surgical extent and individual risk assessment.

Some patients may require liposuction alone, an arm lift alone, or a combined approach.

How Liposuction Is Performed
Liposuction generally involves introducing a thin surgical cannula through small incisions and using suction to remove selected subcutaneous fat.
The precise technique, anesthesia plan, amount of fat to be removed and operative sequence should be determined after assessment rather than standardized from website information.
Liposuction may also be combined with another plastic surgery procedure in appropriately selected patients. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons notes that liposuction can be performed alone or alongside procedures such as a tummy tuck.
When skin excision or abdominal muscle repair is required, the surgery becomes more extensive than liposuction alone and involves different incisions, recovery considerations and risks.

Recovery & Postoperative Care
Recovery varies significantly according to the treated area and the extent of surgery.
Liposuction of one relatively limited area is different from circumferential abdominal liposuction, and combining liposuction with a tummy tuck, arm lift or thigh lift creates a more extensive recovery.
Swelling, bruising, temporary changes in sensation and discomfort can occur after liposuction.
Patients undergoing skin-removal surgery also need to consider incision healing and scar care.
Dr. Maris will discuss postoperative instructions, activity restrictions, follow-up appointments and travel planning according to the actual procedure.

Risks & Considerations
All surgery carries potential risks.
Liposuction risks can include bleeding, infection, fluid accumulation, contour irregularity, asymmetry, changes in sensation, anesthesia-related complications, blood clots and the possible need for revision surgery. More serious complications can occur, although they are less common.
Procedures involving skin removal, including abdominoplasty and body lifts, have additional considerations such as wound-healing problems, seroma, unfavorable scarring, skin loss and persistent contour irregularity.
Your individual risks depend on your medical history, anatomy and proposed operation.
These issues should be discussed directly with Dr. Maris before consent.

What does 360° abdomen liposuction include?

Can I combine 360 liposuction with a tummy tuck?
Potentially, but not every patient should undergo the same combination. Liposuction addresses localized fat, while abdominoplasty is used when excess skin and abdominal wall concerns also need correction. Dr. Maris must assess your anatomy, skin quality, health and planned surgical extent before recommending whether the procedures should be combined.
Is liposuction a way to lose weight?
No. Liposuction is primarily a body-contouring operation rather than a substitute for general weight loss. It is most commonly considered for localized fat deposits in specific areas. If significant weight loss is your primary objective, this should be discussed before cosmetic body-contouring surgery is planned.
Will liposuction tighten loose skin?
Not necessarily. Skin with good elasticity may adapt better after fat removal, while skin with reduced elasticity may remain loose. Significant skin laxity can require a different procedure such as abdominoplasty, brachioplasty or a thigh lift. Dr. Maris evaluates fat and skin separately before recommending surgery.
Can arm liposuction and an arm lift be performed together?
They may be combined in appropriately selected patients when both localized fat and substantial loose skin require correction. However, an arm lift introduces a surgical incision and scar that liposuction alone does not. The decision depends on skin laxity, fat distribution, health and the amount of tissue requiring removal.
