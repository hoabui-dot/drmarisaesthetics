Gastric Sleeve Surgery in Vietnam | DR. MARIS 
Meta Description: Learn about gastric sleeve surgery in Vietnam, including candidacy, hospital care, risks, recovery, long-term follow-up and planning surgery from overseas.
If you are considering surgery because obesity is affecting your health and previous non-surgical approaches have not provided durable results, a gastric sleeve assessment should begin with your medical condition—not simply your target weight. Sleeve gastrectomy is major metabolic surgery and requires appropriate bariatric assessment, hospital care and long-term nutritional follow-up.
Understanding Gastric Sleeve Surgery
Gastric sleeve surgery is the commonly used name for sleeve gastrectomy, also called vertical sleeve gastrectomy.
During the operation, a substantial portion of the stomach is surgically removed, leaving a narrower tube-shaped stomach.
The procedure reduces stomach capacity and also affects hormonal pathways involved in appetite and metabolism. Unlike gastric bypass, sleeve gastrectomy does not normally reroute the small intestine. 
This distinction matters.
Gastric sleeve surgery is not a cosmetic procedure designed simply to make the body appear slimmer. It is a form of metabolic and bariatric surgery used in appropriately selected patients as part of the medical management of obesity and associated health conditions.
Who May Consider Bariatric Surgery?
Eligibility should not be determined from photographs, body weight alone or an online BMI calculator.
Modern bariatric guidelines consider:
BMI
obesity-related medical conditions
metabolic disease
previous attempts at non-surgical weight management
overall surgical risk
nutritional health
psychological readiness
ability to participate in long-term follow-up
The 2022 ASMBS/IFSO guidelines recommend metabolic and bariatric surgery for individuals with BMI of at least approximately 35 kg/m², regardless of the presence or severity of associated conditions, and state that surgery should be considered for patients with metabolic disease and BMI of 30–34.9 kg/m². The same guidelines recognize lower BMI thresholds for Asian populations.
These are professional guidelines, not an automatic indication for surgery.
The final decision should be made by an appropriately qualified bariatric team after clinical assessment.
Obesity-Related Health Conditions
Patients considering metabolic surgery may also have health concerns associated with obesity, such as:
type 2 diabetes
high blood pressure
obstructive sleep apnea
abnormal cholesterol levels
cardiovascular risk
mobility limitations
other obesity-associated metabolic conditions
Sleeve gastrectomy may improve some obesity-related conditions in appropriately selected patients, but individual outcomes vary.
Surgery should not be marketed as a guaranteed cure for diabetes, hypertension, sleep apnea or another medical condition.
Gastric Sleeve Is Not Liposuction or Body Contouring
This distinction is particularly important in the DR. MARIS website.
Liposuction removes selected subcutaneous fat to change body contour.
Sleeve gastrectomy changes the stomach and is performed as metabolic/bariatric surgery for obesity management.
They have fundamentally different:
indications
surgical anatomy
risk profiles
medical teams
recovery pathways
long-term follow-up requirements
Patients should not be encouraged to choose between liposuction and gastric sleeve surgery based on which procedure produces greater weight reduction.
Liposuction is not a substitute for medical obesity management.
How Sleeve Gastrectomy Is Performed
Sleeve gastrectomy is generally performed in a hospital under general anesthesia.
The operation is commonly performed laparoscopically, using several small abdominal incisions. During surgery, the stomach is divided vertically and a large portion is removed, leaving a narrower sleeve-shaped stomach.
The intestines are not normally rerouted as part of a standard sleeve gastrectomy.
The exact surgical technique, stapling system, anesthesia protocol and operative pathway must be determined by the treating bariatric surgeon.
Why Preoperative Assessment Matters
Bariatric surgery should not begin with an operating date.
Assessment may need to review:
height, weight and BMI
previous weight-management history
diabetes and metabolic health
cardiovascular health
sleep apnea
gastrointestinal symptoms
reflux
medications
previous abdominal surgery
smoking and nicotine use
nutritional deficiencies
psychological and behavioral factors
ability to follow postoperative dietary changes
The treating team may require blood tests, imaging, endoscopy or other investigations depending on the patient's medical history.
International patients should establish what testing must be completed before travelling and what needs to be repeated in Vietnam.
Gastric Sleeve and Acid Reflux
Existing reflux symptoms should be discussed before choosing a bariatric procedure.
Gastroesophageal reflux is among the recognized longer-term concerns following sleeve gastrectomy.
For some patients, pre-existing gastrointestinal conditions may influence whether sleeve gastrectomy or another bariatric operation is considered.
This is one reason procedure selection should be made by a bariatric surgeon rather than selecting gastric sleeve solely because it is well known or appears technically simpler.
Risks & Considerations
Sleeve gastrectomy is major abdominal surgery.
Short-term potential risks include:
bleeding
infection
reactions to anesthesia
blood clots
respiratory complications
leakage from the gastric staple line
Longer-term concerns may include:
reflux
gastrointestinal obstruction
hernia
vomiting
nutritional deficiencies
inadequate weight loss
weight regain
need for further medical or surgical management
Mayo Clinic identifies bleeding, infection, blood clots, breathing problems and leakage from the stomach staple line among potential early complications, with reflux and nutritional problems among longer-term considerations.
Rare serious complications can occur.
The risks relevant to an individual patient should be discussed during informed consent with the bariatric surgeon and anesthesia team.
Long-Term Follow-Up Is Part of the Procedure
The operation is only one part of bariatric care.
After sleeve gastrectomy, patients require structured progression of food intake and ongoing monitoring of nutrition and general health.
Long-term care may involve:
dietary guidance
protein and fluid targets
vitamin and mineral supplementation
laboratory monitoring
weight monitoring
management of gastrointestinal symptoms
metabolic follow-up
adjustment of medications
evaluation of insufficient weight loss or weight regain
Mayo Clinic specifically notes the need for ongoing medical checks and nutritional monitoring after sleeve gastrectomy.
For an international patient, this raises an important question:
Who will provide follow-up after you return home?
That plan should be established before travelling for surgery.
Weight Loss & Realistic Expectations
Sleeve gastrectomy can support substantial long-term weight reduction in appropriately selected patients, but the result varies.
Long-term outcome depends on factors including:
starting weight
metabolic health
dietary habits
physical activity
follow-up
hormonal and biological factors
medication
individual response to surgery
Weight regain can occur.
Mayo Clinic notes that inadequate loss or later weight regain is possible, particularly when long-term lifestyle and follow-up requirements are not maintained.
The website should therefore avoid promises such as:
“guaranteed 30 kg loss”
“lose 80% of your weight”
“permanent weight loss”
“never regain weight”
“cure obesity”
A responsible message is that sleeve gastrectomy can be an effective medical tool, but it requires long-term participation from the patient.
Recovery After Gastric Sleeve Surgery
Early recovery begins in the hospital.
Mayo Clinic describes sleeve gastrectomy as typically hospital-based and notes that hospital stay depends on recovery. (Mayo Clinic)
Postoperative care commonly involves:
monitoring for bleeding or leakage
pain management
early mobilization
blood-clot prevention
gradual reintroduction of fluids and food
monitoring hydration
review of wound healing
nutritional instructions
The precise timetable varies between hospitals and patients.
For DR. MARIS content, do not publish a fixed number of hospital days or recovery days until the actual bariatric pathway has been confirmed.
Recovery timing depends on the procedure, medical condition and early postoperative progress and must be discussed with the treating bariatric surgeon.

Cosmetic Surgery After Major Weight Loss
Significant weight loss can leave loose skin around areas such as the abdomen, arms, thighs, breasts or back.
Body-contouring surgery may eventually be considered by selected patients after weight has stabilized and overall health and nutrition are appropriate.
This is a separate stage of care.
Patients should not be encouraged to schedule tummy tuck, extensive liposuction or other major body-contouring surgery automatically alongside sleeve gastrectomy.
Any later cosmetic surgery requires a separate assessment.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is gastric sleeve surgery?
Gastric sleeve surgery, or sleeve gastrectomy, is metabolic and bariatric surgery in which most of the stomach is surgically removed, leaving a narrower sleeve-shaped stomach. It reduces stomach capacity and also changes appetite-related hormonal signaling. Unlike gastric bypass, a standard sleeve procedure does not reroute the intestines. (Mayo Clinic)
Who qualifies for gastric sleeve surgery?
Eligibility depends on BMI, metabolic health, obesity-related conditions, previous non-surgical weight-management attempts and overall medical suitability. Updated ASMBS/IFSO guidance recommends metabolic and bariatric surgery for BMI of approximately 35 kg/m² or above and considers surgery at lower BMI levels in selected patients with metabolic disease. Different thresholds may apply to Asian populations. (PubMed) A bariatric surgeon must determine individual suitability.

Is gastric sleeve surgery cosmetic surgery?
No. Sleeve gastrectomy is metabolic and bariatric surgery used in the medical management of obesity. It is fundamentally different from procedures such as liposuction or abdominoplasty. The operation changes the stomach and requires medical eligibility assessment and long-term follow-up. Cosmetic body-contouring surgery may be considered separately after substantial weight loss in selected patients.
Is the stomach reduction permanent?
The part of the stomach removed during sleeve gastrectomy is not simply restored after recovery, so the anatomical procedure is generally considered irreversible. However, that does not mean weight loss is guaranteed to remain unchanged. Weight regain can occur, and patients require ongoing dietary, medical and lifestyle follow-up. (Mayo Clinic)
What are the main risks of sleeve gastrectomy?
Potential early complications include bleeding, infection, anesthesia complications, blood clots, respiratory problems and leakage from the stomach staple line. Longer-term issues can include reflux, nutritional deficiencies, vomiting, obstruction and inadequate weight loss or weight regain. (Mayo Clinic) Individual risks depend on medical history and should be discussed with the bariatric surgeon.
Can gastric sleeve surgery improve diabetes?
Metabolic and bariatric surgery can improve metabolic disease in appropriately selected patients, including some patients with type 2 diabetes, but improvement or remission should not be guaranteed. Current ASMBS/IFSO guidance specifically recognizes metabolic disease when considering surgery in patients with lower BMI ranges. (PubMed) Diabetes medication and monitoring should be managed by the appropriate medical team.
Will I need vitamins after gastric sleeve surgery?
Nutritional monitoring is an important part of long-term bariatric care, and supplementation may be required. Exact supplementation should be individualized according to the bariatric team's protocol, dietary intake and laboratory results. Mayo Clinic emphasizes ongoing nutritional monitoring and follow-up after sleeve gastrectomy. (Mayo Clinic) Do not self-prescribe supplements based solely on website information.
Can I have a video consultation before flying to Vietnam?
A remote consultation can be useful for preliminary assessment, reviewing medical history and identifying investigations that may be required. However, it cannot replace the in-person examination, anesthesia evaluation or appropriate preoperative testing needed before major abdominal surgery. International patients should also arrange a clear follow-up plan for after they return home.
How long should I stay in Vietnam after gastric sleeve surgery?
The recommended duration cannot be responsibly published until the actual bariatric protocol is confirmed. Stay length should account for hospital recovery, dietary progression, postoperative review, early complications and suitability for long-distance travel. The treating bariatric surgeon should clear the patient for travel rather than relying on a generic medical-tourism timetable.
