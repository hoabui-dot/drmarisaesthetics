Understanding Breast Augmentation
Breast augmentation is surgery performed to increase breast volume and modify breast shape using breast implants.
The decision involves considerably more than selecting an implant size.
A surgical assessment should consider:
chest dimensions
existing breast tissue
skin quality
breast symmetry
nipple position
degree of breast sagging
previous scars
desired change in volume
implant characteristics
long-term expectations
The appropriate implant and surgical plan therefore differ between patients.
Current medical guidance also emphasizes that breast implants are not lifetime devices. Additional surgery may eventually be required because of implant-related complications, changes in breast tissue, or a patient's changing goals.
This should be understood before choosing breast augmentation rather than discovered only after surgery.
Who May Consider Breast Augmentation?
Breast augmentation may be considered by adults seeking to increase breast volume or improve breast proportions after appropriate medical and surgical assessment.
Common reasons for consultation include:
naturally limited breast volume
breast asymmetry
volume changes after pregnancy
changes following weight loss
loss of upper-breast fullness
a desire to change breast proportions
replacement or correction of previous breast implants
However, implants are not necessarily the correct solution for every breast concern.
For example, when significant breast sagging is present, adding volume alone may not adequately reposition the breast. A breast lift may need to be discussed.
Similarly, a patient who already has implants and is experiencing hardening, displacement, pain, unusual breast shape or another problem requires a revision assessment, rather than simply selecting another implant.
Common Breast Concerns We Address
Limited Breast Volume
Breast implants can increase volume when augmentation is anatomically appropriate.
The objective should not be to choose the largest possible implant. Implant selection should consider the patient's tissue, chest proportions and intended result.
Breast Asymmetry
Some degree of natural breast asymmetry is common.
Surgery may improve visible differences in volume or shape, but perfect symmetry cannot be guaranteed. The underlying chest wall, ribs, breast tissue and nipple position may also contribute to asymmetry.
Breast Sagging
Breast ptosis can develop after pregnancy, weight changes, ageing or changes in skin elasticity.
An implant primarily increases volume. It does not automatically correct significant sagging.
Where excess skin and nipple position are important concerns, Breast Lift (Mastopexy) may be more appropriate or may be discussed together with augmentation.

Breast Lift — Mastopexy
A breast lift, medically called mastopexy, addresses breast sagging by removing excess skin and reshaping supporting tissue.
Unlike augmentation, its primary purpose is not simply to increase breast volume.
Professional plastic-surgery guidance describes mastopexy as surgery that raises the breast by removing excess skin and tightening the surrounding tissue.
A breast lift may be considered when:
the breast sits lower than desired
skin has become stretched
nipple position has descended
breast shape has changed after pregnancy or weight loss
an implant alone would not adequately address the concern
Some patients may require a lift without implants.
Others may discuss breast augmentation and breast lift together if both volume and tissue position need to be addressed.
Whether these should be performed together or separately requires clinical assessment.
Symmastia Correction
Symmastia is sometimes described by patients as the breasts or implants appearing to join across the middle of the chest, with reduced separation between them.
It can occur after previous breast augmentation when the medial implant pockets or supporting tissues no longer maintain the intended separation.
Revision planning may need to evaluate:
existing implant position
the previous implant pocket
scar tissue
skin and soft-tissue support
current implant dimensions
previous surgical technique
chest anatomy
Correction is therefore not simply a matter of replacing implants with another pair.
Dr. Maris must examine the existing breast structure and determine what surgical approach, if any, is appropriate.

A patient who has previously undergone silicone injection or another silicone-related procedure requires a different assessment from a routine breast augmentation patient.

The FDA specifically distinguishes injectable silicone from breast implant silicone and notes that injectable silicone is not approved in the United States for breast augmentation or body contouring.

Implant Removal & Capsulectomy
Another revision procedure listed by DR. MARIS is Implant Removal & Capsulectomy.
A capsule is scar tissue that naturally forms around an implant. In some patients, this tissue can tighten around the implant, a condition known as capsular contracture.
The FDA identifies capsular contracture as one of the recognized complications of breast implants and notes that more severe cases may require further surgery.
Depending on the clinical situation, revision surgery may involve:
removing an existing implant
replacing an implant
addressing the surrounding capsule
changing the implant pocket
correcting another associated breast deformity
Not every patient requires the same type or extent of capsulectomy.
Dr. Maris will determine the appropriate approach after examination and review of the previous surgery.

Recovery & Aftercare
Recovery depends on the specific operation.
Primary breast augmentation, breast lift, implant removal, capsulectomy and complex revision surgery do not necessarily follow the same recovery pattern.
Early postoperative experiences can include:
swelling
bruising
discomfort
tightness
temporary changes in breast or nipple sensation
activity limitations
Incision healing and scar maturation also continue beyond the early postoperative period.
For breast lift surgery, wound healing and scar management become particularly important because more skin may need to be surgically reshaped.
For revision surgery, recovery may be affected by scar tissue and the extent of reconstruction required.
Recovery timing depends on the procedure and individual condition and will be discussed during consultation.
International patients should wait for an individualized recommendation before purchasing a fixed return flight.
Risks & Considerations
Breast surgery has potential complications and should not be presented as risk-free.
Recognized breast implant risks include:
capsular contracture
implant rupture
implant displacement
asymmetry
pain
infection
changes in breast or nipple sensation
seroma or fluid accumulation
bleeding or hematoma
scarring
implant visibility or rippling
additional surgery
The FDA also advises patients that breast implants are not lifetime devices and discusses uncommon implant-associated malignancies, including BIA-ALCL and other cancers reported in the capsule surrounding breast implants.
Breast lift surgery carries additional considerations such as wound-healing problems, asymmetry, changes in sensation, unfavorable scarring and other complications associated with tissue reshaping.
Individual risk must be assessed before surgery.
Results & Realistic Expectations
Breast surgery may improve volume, position, shape or symmetry depending on the procedure performed.
It cannot guarantee:
perfect symmetry
a specific cleavage width
an exact bra size
a scar-free result
permanent breast shape
lifetime implant integrity
Breasts continue to change with age, pregnancy, weight fluctuation, tissue stretching and other biological factors.
Breast implants may also require monitoring and future surgery.
A realistic surgical plan should therefore balance the patient's desired change with the limitations of their anatomy and tissue.

Do I need breast augmentation or a breast lift?
It depends on what is causing the concern. Breast augmentation primarily adds volume, while a breast lift addresses significant sagging and excess skin. Some patients may require both. Dr. Maris evaluates breast tissue, nipple position, skin quality and desired volume before recommending whether augmentation, mastopexy or a combined approach should be considered.
Can a breast lift and implants be performed during the same surgery?
They can be combined in selected patients, but this is not appropriate for everyone. The combined operation changes both breast volume and the skin envelope and may involve more extensive surgery than augmentation alone. The decision depends on breast anatomy, tissue quality, health and the degree of lift required.
Can failed breast augmentation be corrected?
Some complications or unwanted results may be suitable for revision surgery, but correction cannot be guaranteed without examination. Dr. Maris needs to assess the implants, implant pockets, capsule, breast tissue, skin, nipple position and previous surgical history before determining what can reasonably be improved.
Can symmastia after breast augmentation be corrected?
Symmastia may be considered for revision surgery, but the appropriate technique depends on why the implants or breast pockets have moved toward the centre of the chest. Existing implant position, scar tissue, tissue support and previous surgery all need to be assessed. DR. MARIS lists Symmastia Correction (Implant Bridging Repair) from 135,000,000 VND.

Can breast implants be removed together with the capsule?
In some cases, implant removal and surgical treatment of the surrounding capsule can be performed during the same operation. However, the extent of capsulectomy depends on the implant, capsule, symptoms and reason for revision. DR. MARIS lists Implant Removal & Capsulectomy from 101,250,000 VND.
Do breast implants need to be replaced every ten years?
There is no universal rule that every breast implant must automatically be replaced at exactly ten years. However, breast implants are not lifetime devices. The FDA advises patients that the likelihood of complications and additional surgery increases over time. Monitoring and future decisions should therefore be based on implant condition, symptoms, imaging where appropriate and medical assessment.
