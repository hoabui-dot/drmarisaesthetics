Understanding Buttock Augmentation
Buttock augmentation is cosmetic surgery intended to change the volume, shape or projection of the buttocks.
Two principal approaches are relevant to this DR. MARIS service page:
Buttock Augmentation – Fat Transfer (BBL)
Buttock Augmentation – Implants
The American Society of Plastic Surgeons describes buttock augmentation as surgery that may use either implants or transfer of the patient's own fat, with the appropriate approach influenced by existing buttock shape, available donor fat, goals, activity level, skin quality and elasticity.
These procedures should not be confused with a buttock lift, which primarily removes or repositions loose tissue rather than simply adding volume.
The consultation therefore begins with a more useful question than “Which procedure gives the biggest buttocks?”

Buttock Augmentation with Fat Transfer — BBL
A Brazilian Butt Lift, commonly shortened to BBL, uses autologous fat transfer.
“Autologous” means the fat comes from the patient's own body.
The procedure generally involves three stages:
Liposuction of Donor Areas
Fat is collected through liposuction from areas where suitable donor fat is available.
Possible donor areas depend on individual anatomy and may include areas such as the abdomen, waist, back or thighs.
Liposuction also affects overall body contour, which means planning should consider the relationship between the waist, hips and buttocks rather than viewing the buttocks in isolation.
Fat Processing
The removed material is prepared so that suitable fat can be used for grafting.
Fat Transfer to the Buttocks
Prepared fat is placed into selected areas to modify contour and projection.
ASPS describes BBL as fat obtained by liposuction, processed and then injected into the buttocks using surgical cannulas.

Patients with very little suitable donor fat may need to discuss whether an implant-based approach is more appropriate.
Buttock Implants
Buttock augmentation can also be performed using implants specifically designed for gluteal augmentation.
Unlike a BBL, this procedure does not depend on harvesting a large amount of fat from another part of the body.
ASPS describes buttock implant surgery as placement of silicone implants through surgically planned incisions, with implant position and surgical approach determined by anatomy, implant selection and surgeon judgment.
Implants may therefore be discussed with patients who want increased projection but do not have enough appropriate donor fat for fat transfer.
However, implant surgery introduces a different set of considerations, including:
a surgical incision and scar
implant positioning
infection risk
wound healing
implant migration or displacement
implant rupture
tissue pressure
possible future revision or removal

Common Concerns We Address
Limited Buttock Projection
Some patients naturally have limited projection despite maintaining a stable body weight and exercising.
Surgery may increase volume, but it cannot guarantee a particular body shape or replicate another person's anatomy.
Changes After Weight Loss
Weight loss can change both buttock volume and skin quality.
If significant loose skin is present, augmentation alone may not address the entire concern.
Buttock Asymmetry
Natural asymmetry is common.
Surgery may improve selected differences, but perfect symmetry cannot be guaranteed because pelvic structure, muscle, fat distribution and soft-tissue anatomy can differ between sides.
Waist-to-Buttock Proportion
Patients considering BBL often focus on the relationship between their waist and buttocks.
Because fat is harvested through liposuction, contour planning may involve both donor and recipient areas. The goal should be individualized body balance rather than a standardized waist measurement or exaggerated silhouette.

BBL Safety Requires Special Attention
A BBL deserves a more detailed safety discussion than ordinary marketing pages often provide.
Multi-society guidance involving ASPS, The Aesthetic Society and other plastic-surgery organizations has recognized gluteal fat grafting as carrying significant risk compared with many other elective aesthetic procedures, particularly because fat entering large blood vessels can cause a potentially fatal fat embolism.
Current safety guidance emphasizes keeping transferred fat in the appropriate subcutaneous plane above the gluteal muscle fascia. A multi-society safety statement also supports real-time ultrasound guidance as a way of helping surgeons verify the anatomical plane during fat injection.
Recent ASPS patient-safety guidance continues to encourage patients considering BBL to discuss the surgeon's injection plane, experience and use of ultrasound guidance.

Recovery & Aftercare
Recovery differs between BBL and implant-based augmentation.
Recovery After BBL
The liposuction donor areas as well as the buttocks need to heal.
ASPS advises that prolonged direct pressure on newly transferred fat is commonly restricted during early recovery and that patients may be instructed to sleep on their side or abdomen and use modified sitting positions.
Specific timing varies between surgeons and patients.
DR. MARIS postoperative restrictions should therefore be published only after they have been medically confirmed.

What is the difference between a BBL and buttock implants?
A BBL uses fat collected from your own body through liposuction and transfers it to the buttocks. Buttock implant surgery uses surgically placed gluteal implants and does not depend on having enough donor fat. Each method has different benefits, limitations, recovery considerations and risks. Dr. Maris should determine the appropriate option after examining your anatomy and goals.
What if I do not have enough fat for a BBL?
A BBL requires sufficient suitable donor fat. Patients with limited donor fat may not be able to achieve the volume they expect through fat transfer alone. An implant-based approach may be discussed in some cases, but the decision depends on buttock anatomy, skin quality, health, desired projection and overall surgical assessment.
Is Brazilian Butt Lift surgery risky?
Yes. BBL has specific risks that require careful discussion. Plastic-surgery societies have highlighted gluteal fat grafting because accidental entry of fat into large blood vessels can cause a potentially fatal fat embolism. Current safety guidance emphasizes appropriate fat-injection planes and supports real-time ultrasound guidance. Patients should ask their surgeon specifically about the safety technique used.
Does all the transferred fat survive after a BBL?
No. Some transferred fat may be reabsorbed during healing, which means the early postoperative size is not the same as the final result. ASPS notes that final results take time to develop and that some initial fat volume is lost as the body reabsorbs it. The amount retained varies between individuals, so a specific final volume should not be guaranteed.
Can BBL be combined with 360° liposuction?
Liposuction is inherently part of fat harvesting for BBL, but whether extensive 360° abdominal liposuction can safely be included in the same surgical plan depends on the patient, surgical extent and medical assessment.
