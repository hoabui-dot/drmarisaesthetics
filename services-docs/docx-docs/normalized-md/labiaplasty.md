## Understanding Intimate Cosmetic Surgery

Female external genital anatomy varies widely between individuals.

Differences in labial length, colour, symmetry and shape can all occur within normal anatomy. A visible or asymmetric labia minora does not automatically indicate a medical problem or a need for surgery.

The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists specifically emphasizes the broad natural variation of female genital anatomy and advises that measurements alone should not determine whether cosmetic genital surgery is appropriate.

For some adults, however, anatomy may be associated with persistent concerns such as:

- friction during exercise
- pulling or twisting of labial tissue
- discomfort in particular clothing
- discomfort during sexual activity
- changes after childbirth
- significant personal concern about external anatomy

In other cases, the concern involves perceived vaginal laxity rather than the external labia.

These are different anatomical problems and require different assessments.

## Labiaplasty — External Genital Contouring

Labiaplasty is surgery involving the labial tissue, most commonly the labia minora.

The American Society of Plastic Surgeons describes labiaplasty as surgery that reduces the length or proportions of the labia minora. Patients may seek assessment because of pulling, twisting, irritation, discomfort during cycling or intercourse, asymmetry, or personal concern regarding appearance.

The purpose of consultation is not to create one standardized genital appearance.

Instead, Dr. Maris evaluates the patient's individual anatomy and asks:

- What specific concern is causing you to consider surgery?
- Is there physical discomfort?
- Is one side significantly different from the other?
- Has the anatomy changed following childbirth or another event?
- Have you undergone previous surgery?
- What result are you expecting?
- Is surgery appropriate for the concern being described?

A medically responsible consultation should also explain that substantial anatomical variation is normal.

## Who May Consider Labiaplasty?

An adult may request a labiaplasty assessment for functional or cosmetic reasons.

Possible concerns can include:

### Persistent Friction or Irritation

Some patients experience irritation when labial tissue rubs against clothing or during physical activities.

### Pulling or Twisting During Exercise

Cycling, running or other activities may cause discomfort in some individuals when tissue is repeatedly compressed or pulled.

### Discomfort During Intimacy

Labial tissue may occasionally be associated with pulling or discomfort during sexual activity.

This requires sensitive assessment because pain during intercourse can have many other causes and should not automatically be attributed to the labia.

### Significant Asymmetry

One side of the labia can naturally be longer than the other. Surgery should only be considered if the patient understands that asymmetry itself is common and still wishes to discuss alteration after informed assessment.

### Changes After Childbirth

Pregnancy and childbirth can alter genital and perineal tissues. The exact concern should be identified before determining whether labiaplasty, another procedure, or no cosmetic surgery is appropriate.

## What Labiaplasty Does — and Does Not Do

Labiaplasty primarily changes external labial tissue.

It should not be marketed as a procedure guaranteed to:

- improve sexual pleasure
- increase orgasm
- prevent infection
- correct urinary problems
- make genital anatomy medically “normal”
- produce perfect symmetry

Evidence supporting claims that elective cosmetic genital surgery consistently improves sexual function remains limited.

ACOG specifically advises patients that high-quality evidence on the effectiveness of elective female genital cosmetic surgery is limited and that counseling should address uncertainty as well as potential risks.

For that reason, DR. MARIS content should focus on anatomy, physical concerns, informed choice and realistic expectations rather than promising improved sexuality.

## Vaginal Tightening — Vaginoplasty

Vaginal tightening surgery, also called **vaginoplasty** in the DR. MARIS price list, addresses the vaginal canal rather than primarily reshaping the external labia.

The American Society of Plastic Surgeons describes aesthetic vaginoplasty as a surgical procedure intended to reduce vaginal laxity by addressing vaginal tissue and, depending on the technique, deeper supporting tissues.

Some adults seek consultation after childbirth or because they perceive a change in vaginal tightness.

However, the phrase **“vaginal looseness”** can describe several different situations.

Symptoms can potentially relate to:

- vaginal tissue changes
- pelvic floor weakness
- childbirth injury
- pelvic organ prolapse
- urinary symptoms
- pain
- other gynecological or pelvic-floor conditions

Cosmetic surgery should therefore not be assumed to be the correct solution before medical assessment.

If symptoms suggest a gynecological, pelvic-floor, prolapse or continence problem, additional specialist evaluation may be necessary.

## Why Assessment Matters Before Intimate Surgery

Intimate cosmetic surgery requires particularly careful informed consent.

ACOG notes that the evidence base for elective female genital cosmetic surgery remains limited and recommends counseling patients about both natural anatomical variation and potential complications.

This means consultation should not assume:

**“You have abnormal anatomy, so you need surgery.”**

A more medically appropriate approach is:

**“You have a concern. We need to understand your anatomy, symptoms, expectations and the available evidence before determining whether surgery is appropriate.”**

This approach protects patient autonomy and reduces unrealistic expectations.

## Previous Surgery or Complex Cases

Patients may also seek consultation after a previous labiaplasty or other intimate procedure.

Potential concerns after previous surgery may include:

- persistent asymmetry
- excessive tissue removal
- insufficient reduction
- uncomfortable scar tissue
- altered sensation
- wound-healing problems
- pain
- an unwanted cosmetic result

Revision surgery can be more complex because previous procedures may alter tissue availability, blood supply and scar patterns.

Cleveland Clinic identifies over-removal or under-removal of tissue, wound breakdown, scarring, persistent pain and sensitivity changes among potential labiaplasty complications.

A revision result cannot be promised without examination.

## Recovery & Aftercare

Recovery depends on the operation performed and the individual patient's healing.

### After Labiaplasty

Swelling, tenderness, bruising and minor bleeding can occur during early recovery.

Cleveland Clinic notes that patients are generally advised to limit strenuous activity while healing, with sexual activity and activities that place pressure on the surgical site commonly restricted for several weeks.

ASPS similarly describes activity restrictions during the healing period after labiaplasty.

These general timelines should not be presented as a guaranteed DR. MARIS recovery protocol.

### After Vaginoplasty

Recovery can be more extensive because the surgery involves vaginal tissues rather than only external labial tissue.

ASPS notes that restrictions on tampon use and sexual intercourse may extend for several weeks after surgical vaginoplasty.

**Recovery timing depends on the procedure and individual condition and will be discussed during consultation.**

## Risks & Considerations

All surgery carries potential complications.

ACOG lists potential complications of female genital cosmetic surgery including:

- pain
- bleeding
- infection
- scarring
- adhesions
- altered sensation
- pain during intercourse
- need for additional surgery

Potential labiaplasty-specific concerns also include:

- hematoma
- wound separation
- excessive tissue removal
- insufficient tissue removal
- persistent discomfort
- sensitivity changes

Vaginoplasty also carries risks including bleeding, infection and pain, along with procedure-specific complications that should be discussed during informed consent.

Individual risk depends on health, anatomy, previous procedures, surgical technique and other factors.

## Results & Realistic Expectations

Intimate cosmetic surgery should not be used to create an artificial definition of what female genital anatomy is “supposed” to look like.

There is substantial normal variation.

Surgery may alter selected tissues, but it cannot guarantee:

- perfect symmetry
- invisible scars
- a particular sexual response
- increased orgasm
- permanent vaginal tightness
- a specific appearance
- freedom from future anatomical changes

Pregnancy, childbirth, ageing, tissue elasticity and other factors can affect anatomy over time.

Results vary between patients.

For purely cosmetic genital surgery, patients should also understand that high-quality evidence demonstrating improvements in sexual function or psychological outcomes remains limited.

### Is it normal for one labia to be larger than the other?

Yes. Differences in labial size, length and symmetry are common, and normal female genital anatomy varies considerably. ACOG advises that anatomical measurements alone should not be used to determine whether cosmetic surgery is appropriate. If asymmetry causes physical discomfort or remains an important concern after understanding normal variation, a consultation can explore possible options.

### Can labiaplasty help with physical discomfort?

It may help selected patients whose labial anatomy contributes to persistent pulling, twisting, friction or irritation. ASPS lists discomfort during activities and intercourse among reasons some patients consider labiaplasty. However, symptoms should be evaluated rather than automatically attributed to labial anatomy, particularly when pain, urinary symptoms or other gynecological concerns are present.

### Will labiaplasty improve sexual pleasure?

It should not be promised. Evidence that cosmetic labiaplasty consistently improves sexual function is limited. ACOG advises that high-quality evidence supporting improvements in sexual function from cosmetic genital surgery is lacking. The purpose of surgery should be clearly defined during consultation, and patients should understand that changes in sensation or pain during intercourse are also potential complications.

### What is the difference between labiaplasty and vaginal tightening?

Labiaplasty changes external labial tissue, usually the labia minora. Vaginal tightening or vaginoplasty addresses the vaginal canal and supporting tissues. They therefore address different anatomical concerns. A patient seeking surgery after childbirth may potentially have external changes, vaginal laxity, pelvic-floor symptoms or a combination, so direct assessment is important before selecting a procedure.
