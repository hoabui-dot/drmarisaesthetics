## Understanding Facelift Surgery

A facelift, medically known as **rhytidectomy**, is a surgical procedure designed to improve visible ageing changes in the face and, depending on the surgical plan, portions of the neck.

The American Society of Plastic Surgeons describes concerns commonly addressed by facelift surgery as facial skin relaxation, deeper facial folds, changes in facial fat, jowls and loose skin or excess fat around the neck.

A facelift is not simply a procedure to stretch the surface of the skin.

Facial ageing can involve several anatomical changes at the same time:

- skin becomes looser
- soft tissues may descend
- jowls may become more visible
- jawline definition can change
- facial volume distribution can change
- the neck may develop laxity
- forehead and brow position may descend

Because these changes occur differently between patients, the appropriate surgery should be selected after examining the **face as a whole**.

Some patients may be suitable for a more limited mini facelift.

Others may have significant neck ageing and require a neck lift.

For another patient, descent of the forehead or eyebrows may be more important than lower-face laxity, making a forehead or brow lift a separate consideration.

## Who May Consider Facelift Surgery?

Facelift surgery may be considered by adults experiencing visible facial or neck ageing who are medically suitable for elective surgery and have realistic expectations.

Common concerns include:

- early or moderate jowling
- loose skin around the lower face
- reduced jawline definition
- deeper facial folds
- sagging around the cheek or jaw
- loose neck skin
- ageing changes affecting both face and neck
- lowered eyebrow position
- forehead laxity

Age alone does not determine whether someone needs a facelift.

Two patients of the same age may have very different skin quality, facial structure, soft-tissue descent and surgical needs.

General medical guidance also considers overall health, smoking status and realistic expectations when evaluating facelift candidates.

## Common Concerns We Address

### Jowls and Loss of Jawline Definition

One of the common reasons patients begin researching facelift surgery is increasing heaviness or laxity around the lower face.

As facial tissues change, the transition between the cheek, jaw and neck can become less defined.

The appropriate surgery depends on where the laxity originates. A limited lower-face procedure may be considered in some cases, while more extensive facial or neck aging can require a different approach.

### Loose Facial Skin

Visible laxity cannot always be evaluated by looking at skin alone.

Underlying tissue position, skin elasticity, previous procedures and facial structure can all affect the surgical plan.

### Neck Laxity

The neck often ages differently from the central face.

Mayo Clinic describes neck lift surgery as a cosmetic operation addressing excess skin and fat around the jawline and notes that it is frequently performed as part of facial rejuvenation surgery.

When significant neck laxity is present, treating only the face may not adequately address the patient's concern.

### Brow and Forehead Descent

A low or descending brow can contribute to a tired or heavy appearance around the upper face.

A brow lift—also called a forehead lift—is a separate surgical procedure from a facelift. It addresses the forehead and brow region rather than primarily treating the lower face and jawline.

## Mini Facelift

A **mini facelift** is generally used to describe a more limited facial lifting procedure than a traditional full facelift.

It may be considered when ageing changes are relatively localized and a full facial rejuvenation operation is not required.

The term “mini facelift,” however, does not describe one universally standardized surgical technique.

Incision length, tissue dissection and the anatomical areas treated can differ between surgeons.

For this reason, the important question is not simply:

**“Can I have a mini facelift?”**

It is:

**“Which areas actually need surgical correction?”**

A patient with moderate lower-face laxity may have very different needs from someone with extensive jowling and neck laxity.

Dr. Maris therefore assesses:

- facial skin laxity
- location of tissue descent
- jawline
- jowls
- neck condition
- skin quality
- previous facial procedures
- hairline and scar considerations
- overall facial proportions

before determining whether a limited procedure is appropriate.

## Mini Facelift vs Full Facelift

Patients frequently search for a mini facelift because they expect smaller scars, less surgery or faster recovery.

The distinction should instead begin with anatomy.

### A Mini Facelift May Be Considered When

Ageing changes are relatively limited and concentrated in specific parts of the face.

### A More Extensive Facelift May Be Considered When

There is greater facial laxity, more significant jowling or ageing changes involving broader areas of the face.

### A Neck Lift May Also Be Required When

The neck has substantial loose skin, fat distribution changes or underlying tissue laxity that will not be sufficiently addressed through facial surgery alone.

The American Society of Plastic Surgeons recognizes traditional facelift, limited-incision facelift and neck lift as different surgical approaches selected according to the degree of correction required.

A smaller operation is not automatically better.

The appropriate operation is the one that matches the anatomical problem while maintaining acceptable surgical risk.

## Forehead Lift / Brow Lift

A **forehead lift**, also commonly called a **brow lift**, addresses ageing changes in the upper face.

It is different from a lower-face facelift.

The American Society of Plastic Surgeons describes brow lift surgery as a procedure used to reduce forehead wrinkles, improve frown-line concerns and reposition the eyebrows.

Patients may consider a forehead or brow assessment when they are concerned about:

- lowered eyebrow position
- heaviness around the brow
- forehead laxity
- upper-face ageing
- asymmetry of brow position

It is particularly important to distinguish brow descent from excess upper-eyelid skin.

In some patients, the apparent heaviness around the eyes involves the brow, the eyelid, or both.

This is one reason Dr. Maris evaluates the relationship between the forehead, eyebrows and eyelids before recommending surgery.

## How Facelift Surgery Is Planned

Facelift surgery involves repositioning and managing facial tissues through surgically planned incisions.

The exact technique can vary substantially according to anatomy and the extent of correction.

Professional plastic-surgery guidance recognizes that facelift surgery may involve different approaches to the skin and deeper supporting tissues.

A surgical plan may need to consider:

### Incision Placement

Incisions are planned to provide access to the tissues requiring correction while considering scar position and surrounding anatomy.

### Skin and Underlying Tissue

The face should not simply be treated as a loose layer of skin.

Deeper supporting tissues may also require surgical management depending on the selected technique.

### Neck Anatomy

When neck ageing contributes significantly to the overall appearance, separate or combined neck surgery may be considered.

### Brow Position

Lower-face surgery does not automatically correct a descending brow. Upper-face concerns may require separate evaluation.

### Existing Asymmetry

Natural facial asymmetry should be identified before surgery. Surgery may improve certain asymmetries but cannot guarantee perfect symmetry.

## Recovery & Aftercare

Recovery varies according to whether the patient undergoes:

- mini facelift
- more extensive facelift surgery
- neck lift
- forehead lift
- combined facial procedures
- revision surgery

Swelling and bruising are expected after many facial surgical procedures.

Temporary tightness, numbness and changes in skin sensation can also occur.

The American Society of Plastic Surgeons notes that postoperative dressings may be used after facelift surgery and that temporary drains may sometimes be required. Patients receive specific instructions regarding incision care, medications, activity and follow-up.

Although ASPS notes that many facelift patients may be socially presentable at approximately 10–14 days, settling of sensation, texture and tightness can continue for substantially longer.

This general timeline should **not** be treated as a guaranteed DR. MARIS recovery schedule.

**Recovery timing depends on the procedure and individual condition and will be discussed during consultation.**

## Risks & Considerations

All facelift and facial rejuvenation surgery carries potential risks.

Recognized facelift risks include:

- anesthesia complications
- bleeding
- hematoma or fluid accumulation
- infection
- poor wound healing
- skin loss
- unfavorable scarring
- temporary or permanent changes in sensation
- facial nerve injury or weakness
- facial asymmetry
- prolonged swelling
- hair loss around incision areas
- persistent pain
- unsatisfactory results
- possible need for revision surgery
- blood clots and cardiopulmonary complications

These risks are described in professional facelift safety guidance and should be discussed as part of informed consent.

Brow lift surgery has its own risk profile, including potential brow asymmetry, hairline changes, hair loss near incisions, sensation changes, nerve injury, unfavorable scarring and possible revision surgery.

Individual risk depends on medical history, anatomy, smoking status, procedure extent and other factors.

## Results & Realistic Expectations

Facelift surgery may improve visible signs of facial aging, but it does not stop the biological aging process.

ASPS specifically notes that a facelift is a restorative procedure and cannot stop future ageing or fundamentally change a patient's underlying appearance.

Results also cannot guarantee:

- perfect symmetry
- complete removal of every wrinkle
- invisible scars
- an exact number of years of apparent age reduction
- permanent facial tightness
- a specific jawline
- identical results to another patient

A responsible goal is improvement that is appropriate to the patient's starting anatomy.

Changes after surgery continue to be influenced by:

- ageing
- skin quality
- genetics
- sun exposure
- smoking
- weight changes
- tissue characteristics

Results vary between patients.

### What is the difference between a mini facelift and a full facelift?

However, “mini facelift” is not one standardized surgical technique. ### Can a mini facelift improve jowls?

A mini facelift may improve selected lower-face concerns in appropriately assessed patients, including certain degrees of jowling. It may not be sufficient when facial laxity is extensive or when the neck is also significantly affected. The appropriate procedure depends on where the tissue has descended and how much correction is required.

### Do I need a facelift and neck lift together?

Not everyone requires both. Some patients have primarily facial laxity, while others have more significant changes in the neck. A combined approach may be considered when ageing affects both areas and treating only one would provide an incomplete correction. Dr. Maris evaluates the face, jawline and neck together before recommending a surgical plan.

### What is the difference between a brow lift and a facelift?

A facelift primarily addresses ageing of the face and potentially portions of the neck, depending on technique. A **brow or forehead lift** addresses the upper face, especially brow position and forehead concerns. If heaviness around the eyes is caused partly by a low brow rather than only excess eyelid skin, a brow assessment may be particularly relevant.

### Will facelift scars be visible?

Facelift surgery requires surgical incisions, so scars cannot truthfully be described as nonexistent. The location and length of scars depend on the selected procedure and technique. Scars generally change during healing, but final visibility varies with incision placement, tissue tension, individual healing and other factors. Unfavorable scarring is also a recognized potential complication of facelift surgery.

### How long does facelift recovery take?

Recovery differs between procedures and patients. Swelling, bruising, tightness and temporary sensation changes can occur during early healing. ASPS notes that many facelift patients may be socially presentable around 10–14 days, while tissue sensation and tightness may continue settling for several months. Your actual recovery schedule should be provided by Dr. Maris after the surgical plan is determined.
