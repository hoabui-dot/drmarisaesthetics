# SECTION 02 — UNDERSTANDING FACELIFT SURGERY

## Component Type

Educational / procedure overview section

## Heading

Understanding Facelift Surgery

## Content

A facelift, medically known as rhytidectomy, is a surgical procedure designed to improve visible ageing changes in the face and, depending on the surgical plan, portions of the neck.

The American Society of Plastic Surgeons describes concerns commonly addressed by facelift surgery as facial skin relaxation, deeper facial folds, changes in facial fat, jowls and loose skin or excess fat around the neck.

A facelift is not simply a procedure to stretch the surface of the skin.

Facial ageing can involve several anatomical changes at the same time:

- skin becomes looser
- soft tissues may descend
- jowls may become more visible
- jawline definition can change
- facial volume distribution can change
- the neck may develop laxity
- forehead and brow position may descend

Because these changes occur differently between patients, the appropriate surgery should be selected after examining the face as a whole.

Some patients may be suitable for a more limited mini facelift.

Others may have significant neck ageing and require a neck lift.

For another patient, descent of the forehead or eyebrows may be more important than lower-face laxity, making a forehead or brow lift a separate consideration.

## Core Message

Facial rejuvenation should be selected according to anatomy rather than selecting a procedure based only on its name.

# SECTION 04 — COMMON CONCERNS WE ADDRESS

## Component Type

Concern cards / anatomical problem section

## Heading

Common Concerns We Address

### 4.1 Jowls and Loss of Jawline Definition

One of the common reasons patients begin researching facelift surgery is increasing heaviness or laxity around the lower face.

As facial tissues change, the transition between the cheek, jaw and neck can become less defined.

The appropriate surgery depends on where the laxity originates.

A limited lower-face procedure may be considered in some cases, while more extensive facial or neck aging can require a different approach.

### 4.2 Loose Facial Skin

Visible laxity cannot always be evaluated by looking at skin alone.

Factors affecting the surgical plan include:

- underlying tissue position
- skin elasticity
- previous procedures
- facial structure

### 4.3 Neck Laxity

The neck often ages differently from the central face.

Mayo Clinic describes neck lift surgery as a cosmetic operation addressing excess skin and fat around the jawline and notes that it is frequently performed as part of facial rejuvenation surgery.

When significant neck laxity is present, treating only the face may not adequately address the patient's concern.

### 4.4 Brow and Forehead Descent

A low or descending brow can contribute to a tired or heavy appearance around the upper face.

A brow lift, also called a forehead lift, is a separate surgical procedure from a facelift.

It addresses the forehead and brow region rather than primarily treating the lower face and jawline.

# SECTION 05 — MINI FACELIFT

## Component Type

Procedure detail section

## Heading

Mini Facelift

## Content

A mini facelift is generally used to describe a more limited facial lifting procedure than a traditional full facelift.

It may be considered when ageing changes are relatively localized and a full facial rejuvenation operation is not required.

The term "mini facelift," however, does not describe one universally standardized surgical technique.

Incision length, tissue dissection and the anatomical areas treated can differ between surgeons.

## Key Question

The important question is not simply:

> "Can I have a mini facelift?"

It is:

> "Which areas actually need surgical correction?"

A patient with moderate lower-face laxity may have very different needs from someone with extensive jowling and neck laxity.

## What Dr. Maris Assesses

Dr. Maris therefore assesses:

- facial skin laxity
- location of tissue descent
- jawline
- jowls
- neck condition
- skin quality
- previous facial procedures
- hairline and scar considerations
- overall facial proportions

before determining whether a limited procedure is appropriate.

# SECTION 06 — MINI FACELIFT VS FULL FACELIFT

## Component Type

Comparison section

## Heading

Mini Facelift vs Full Facelift

## Intro

Patients frequently search for a mini facelift because they expect smaller scars, less surgery or faster recovery.

The distinction should instead begin with anatomy.

### Mini Facelift May Be Considered When

Ageing changes are relatively limited and concentrated in specific parts of the face.

### A More Extensive Facelift May Be Considered When

There is greater facial laxity, more significant jowling or ageing changes involving broader areas of the face.

### A Neck Lift May Also Be Required When

The neck has substantial:

- loose skin
- fat distribution changes
- underlying tissue laxity

that will not be sufficiently addressed through facial surgery alone.

## Important Principle

The American Society of Plastic Surgeons recognizes traditional facelift, limited-incision facelift and neck lift as different surgical approaches selected according to the degree of correction required.

A smaller operation is not automatically better.

The appropriate operation is the one that matches the anatomical problem while maintaining acceptable surgical risk.

Not necessarily.

The face and neck should therefore be assessed together rather than automatically sold as a combined package.

A combined surgical plan may be considered when treating one area alone would leave a visible imbalance between the:

- face
- jawline
- neck

## Decision Factors

The decision depends on:

- location of laxity
- neck anatomy
- skin quality
- facial proportions
- medical suitability
- extent of surgery required

Dr. Maris will assess whether one procedure or a combined approach is appropriate.

# SECTION 09 — FOREHEAD LIFT / BROW LIFT

## Component Type

Procedure detail section

## Heading

Forehead Lift / Brow Lift

## Content

A forehead lift, also commonly called a brow lift, addresses ageing changes in the upper face.

It is different from a lower-face facelift.

The American Society of Plastic Surgeons describes brow lift surgery as a procedure used to:

- reduce forehead wrinkles
- improve frown-line concerns
- reposition the eyebrows

## Patients May Consider Assessment For

- lowered eyebrow position
- heaviness around the brow
- forehead laxity
- upper-face ageing
- asymmetry of brow position

## Brow vs Eyelid Assessment

It is particularly important to distinguish brow descent from excess upper-eyelid skin.

In some patients, the apparent heaviness around the eyes involves:

- the brow
- the eyelid
- or both

This is one reason Dr. Maris evaluates the relationship between the forehead, eyebrows and eyelids before recommending surgery.

# SECTION 11 — HOW FACELIFT SURGERY IS PLANNED

## Component Type

Process / surgical-planning section

## Heading

How Facelift Surgery Is Planned

## Intro

Facelift surgery involves repositioning and managing facial tissues through surgically planned incisions.

The exact technique can vary substantially according to anatomy and the extent of correction.

Professional plastic-surgery guidance recognizes that facelift surgery may involve different approaches to the skin and deeper supporting tissues.

### Incision Placement

Incisions are planned to provide access to the tissues requiring correction while considering:

- scar position
- surrounding anatomy

### Skin and Underlying Tissue

The face should not simply be treated as a loose layer of skin.

Deeper supporting tissues may also require surgical management depending on the selected technique.

### Neck Anatomy

When neck ageing contributes significantly to the overall appearance, separate or combined neck surgery may be considered.

### Brow Position

Lower-face surgery does not automatically correct a descending brow.

Upper-face concerns may require separate evaluation.

### Existing Asymmetry

Natural facial asymmetry should be identified before surgery.

Surgery may improve certain asymmetries but cannot guarantee perfect symmetry.

Swelling and bruising are expected after many facial surgical procedures.

Temporary symptoms can also include:

- tightness

- incision care
- medications

Risks & Considerations

## Intro

All facelift and facial rejuvenation surgery carries potential risks.

## Recognized Facelift Risks

- anesthesia complications
- bleeding
- hematoma or fluid accumulation
- infection
- poor wound healing
- skin loss
- unfavorable scarring

- anatomy

# SECTION 18 — RESULTS & REALISTIC EXPECTATIONS

## Component Type

Outcome expectations section

## Heading

Results & Realistic Expectations

## Content

Facelift surgery may improve visible signs of facial aging, but it does not stop the biological aging process.

ASPS specifically notes that a facelift is a restorative procedure and cannot stop future ageing or fundamentally change a patient's underlying appearance.

## Results Cannot Guarantee

- perfect symmetry
- complete removal of every wrinkle
- invisible scars
- an exact number of years of apparent age reduction
- permanent facial tightness
- a specific jawline
- identical results to another patient

## Appropriate Goal

A responsible goal is improvement that is appropriate to the patient's starting anatomy.

## Factors Affecting Long-Term Changes

Changes after surgery continue to be influenced by:

- ageing
- skin quality
- genetics
- sun exposure
- smoking
- weight changes
- tissue characteristics

Results vary between patients.

### FAQ 2 — What is the difference between a mini facelift and a full facelift?

A mini facelift generally refers to a more limited operation for selected facial ageing concerns, while a more extensive facelift can address broader tissue laxity.

However, "mini facelift" is not one standardized surgical technique.

### FAQ 3 — Can a mini facelift improve jowls?

A mini facelift may improve selected lower-face concerns in appropriately assessed patients, including certain degrees of jowling.

It may not be sufficient when:

- facial laxity is extensive
- the neck is also significantly affected

The appropriate procedure depends on where the tissue has descended and how much correction is required.

---

### FAQ 4 — Do I need a facelift and neck lift together?

Not everyone requires both.

Some patients have primarily facial laxity, while others have more significant changes in the neck.

A combined approach may be considered when ageing affects both areas and treating only one would provide an incomplete correction.

Dr. Maris evaluates the face, jawline and neck together before recommending a surgical plan.

---

### FAQ 5 — What is the difference between a brow lift and a facelift?

A facelift primarily addresses ageing of the face and potentially portions of the neck, depending on technique.

A brow or forehead lift addresses the upper face, especially:

- brow position
- forehead concerns

If heaviness around the eyes is caused partly by a low brow rather than only excess eyelid skin, a brow assessment may be particularly relevant.

---

### FAQ 6 — Will facelift scars be visible?

Facelift surgery requires surgical incisions, so scars cannot truthfully be described as nonexistent.

The location and length of scars depend on the selected procedure and technique.

Scars generally change during healing, but final visibility varies with:

- incision placement
- tissue tension
- individual healing
- other factors

Unfavorable scarring is also a recognized potential complication of facelift surgery.

---

### FAQ 7 — How long does facelift recovery take?

Recovery differs between procedures and patients.

Early healing can include:

- swelling
- bruising
- tightness
- temporary sensation changes

ASPS notes that many facelift patients may be socially presentable around 10–14 days, while tissue sensation and tightness may continue settling for several months.

Your actual recovery schedule should be provided by Dr. Maris after the surgical plan is determined.

---

### FAQ 8 — How long should I stay in Vietnam after facelift surgery?

There is no single appropriate stay for every patient.

A:

- mini facelift
- combined face-and-neck operation
- brow lift
- revision procedure

can require different postoperative schedules.

Your stay should allow appropriate reviews and early recovery before a long international flight.

Dr. Maris should confirm the recommended duration before you book return travel.

### FAQ 10 — Does a facelift stop facial ageing permanently?

No.

A facelift may reposition tissues and improve visible ageing changes, but it cannot stop the ageing process.

Skin, soft tissues and facial structure will continue to change over time.

The aim should therefore be realistic improvement rather than a guarantee of:

- permanent facial tightness
- a specific number of years of rejuvenation
