# SECTION 02 — UNDERSTANDING LIPOSUCTION & BODY CONTOURING

## Component Type

Educational / procedure overview section

## Heading

Understanding Liposuction and Body Contouring

## Content

Liposuction is a surgical procedure used to remove localized fat deposits and reshape specific areas of the body.

Common areas include:

- abdomen
- waist
- back
- upper arms
- thighs
- chin

Liposuction should not be considered a substitute for weight loss.

Mayo Clinic and the American Society of Plastic Surgeons describe liposuction primarily as a body-contouring procedure for localized fat rather than an obesity or general weight-loss treatment.

## Key Planning Principle

An important part of planning is determining what is creating the contour concern.

### When Localized Fat Is the Main Concern

For some patients, the main issue is excess fat with relatively good skin elasticity.

Liposuction alone may therefore be considered.

### When Loose Skin or Tissue Laxity Is the Main Concern

For other patients, significant:

- skin laxity
- tissue redundancy
- abdominal wall changes

mean that removing fat alone may not provide the desired contour.

A procedure such as:

- tummy tuck
- arm lift
- thigh lift

may need to be considered instead of — or in selected cases together with — liposuction.

## Core Message

Fat removal and skin removal solve different anatomical problems.

The distinction should be made after clinical assessment.

# SECTION 05 — 360° LIPOSUCTION OR TUMMY TUCK?

## Component Type

Procedure comparison / decision-support section

## Heading

360° Liposuction or Tummy Tuck?

## Intro

These procedures address different anatomical concerns.

### 360° Liposuction Primarily Addresses

Localized fat.

### Abdominoplasty Primarily Addresses

Excess abdominal skin and may include repair or tightening of weakened abdominal musculature when clinically appropriate.

The American Society of Plastic Surgeons describes abdominoplasty as a procedure that removes excess skin and fat while restoring weakened abdominal muscles.

## Important Distinction

If significant loose skin is present, increasing the amount of liposuction does not necessarily solve the underlying skin problem.

## Possible Combined Approach

For some patients, Dr. Maris may therefore discuss:

**360° abdomen liposuction + abdominoplasty + muscle repair**

Whether these procedures should be combined depends on:

- anatomy
- health
- surgical extent
- individual risk assessment

## Decision Rule

Do not recommend liposuction solely because abdominal fat is present.

Skin quality and abdominal wall condition must also be assessed.

# SECTION 06 — ARM LIPOSUCTION & ARM LIFT

## Component Type

Procedure comparison section

## Heading

Arm Liposuction & Arm Lift

## Intro

Upper-arm contour concerns may result from:

- excess fat
- loose skin
- a combination of both

### Arm Liposuction

Arm liposuction removes selected subcutaneous fat to refine upper-arm proportions when skin quality is suitable.

### Arm Lift — Brachioplasty

When substantial loose skin is present, an arm lift or brachioplasty may be considered.

An arm lift involves surgical removal and reshaping of excess tissue.

Because skin is removed:

- an incision is required
- a resulting scar is expected

The appropriate incision pattern depends on how much tissue requires correction and must be discussed during consultation.

## Possible Treatment Plans

Depending on anatomy, a patient may require:

- arm liposuction alone
- arm lift alone
- combined arm liposuction + arm lift

## Core Message

Fat volume and skin laxity must be assessed separately.

# SECTION 12 — HOW LIPOSUCTION IS PERFORMED

## Component Type

Procedure explanation section

## Heading

How Liposuction Is Performed

## Content

Liposuction generally involves introducing a thin surgical cannula through small incisions and using suction to remove selected subcutaneous fat.

## Individualized Surgical Planning

The following should be determined after assessment rather than standardized from website information:

- precise technique
- anesthesia plan
- amount of fat to be removed
- operative sequence

## Combined Surgery

Liposuction may also be combined with another plastic surgery procedure in appropriately selected patients.

The American Society of Plastic Surgeons notes that liposuction can be performed alone or alongside procedures such as a tummy tuck.

## Important Distinction

When skin excision or abdominal muscle repair is required, surgery becomes more extensive than liposuction alone.

This involves different:

- incisions
- recovery considerations
- risks

# SECTION 15 — RISKS & CONSIDERATIONS

## Component Type

Medical risk disclosure

## Heading

Risks & Considerations

## Intro

All surgery carries potential risks.

## Liposuction Risks Can Include

- bleeding
- infection
- fluid accumulation
- contour irregularity
- asymmetry
- changes in sensation
- anesthesia-related complications
- blood clots
- possible need for revision surgery

More serious complications can occur, although they are less common.

## Additional Risks for Skin-Removal Procedures

Procedures involving skin removal, including abdominoplasty and body lifts, have additional considerations such as:

- wound-healing problems
- seroma
- unfavorable scarring
- skin loss
- persistent contour irregularity

## Individual Risk

Individual risks depend on:

- medical history
- anatomy
- proposed operation

These issues should be discussed directly with Dr. Maris before consent.

Results & Realistic Expectations

Liposuction can reduce fat cells within selected areas and change body contour, but it does not prevent future weight gain.

### FAQ 2 — What does 360° abdomen liposuction include?

The DR. MARIS international price list defines 360° abdomen liposuction as contouring of the:

- front abdomen
- waist
- lower back

The precise treatment areas and amount of fat removal still depend on your anatomy.

The aim is circumferential torso contouring rather than treating only the front of the abdomen.

---

### FAQ 3 — Can I combine 360 liposuction with a tummy tuck?

Potentially, but not every patient should undergo the same combination.

Liposuction addresses localized fat.

Abdominoplasty is used when excess skin and abdominal wall concerns also need correction.

Dr. Maris must assess:

- anatomy
- skin quality
- health
- planned surgical extent

before recommending whether the procedures should be combined.

---

### FAQ 4 — Is liposuction a way to lose weight?

No.

Liposuction is primarily a body-contouring operation rather than a substitute for general weight loss.

It is most commonly considered for localized fat deposits in specific areas.

If significant weight loss is your primary objective, this should be discussed before cosmetic body-contouring surgery is planned.

---

### FAQ 5 — Will liposuction tighten loose skin?

Not necessarily.

Skin with good elasticity may adapt better after fat removal, while skin with reduced elasticity may remain loose.

Significant skin laxity can require a different procedure such as:

- abdominoplasty
- brachioplasty
- thigh lift

Dr. Maris evaluates fat and skin separately before recommending surgery.

---

### FAQ 6 — Can arm liposuction and an arm lift be performed together?

They may be combined in appropriately selected patients when both localized fat and substantial loose skin require correction.

However, an arm lift introduces a surgical incision and scar that liposuction alone does not.

The decision depends on:

- skin laxity
- fat distribution
- health
- amount of tissue requiring removal

---

### FAQ 7 — How long should I stay in Ho Chi Minh City after liposuction?

There is no single recommended stay for every patient.

Required recovery time depends on:

- areas treated
- scale of liposuction
- whether additional procedures are performed

Additional procedures may include:

- tummy tuck
- arm lift
- thigh lift

Dr. Maris should confirm an appropriate postoperative schedule before you finalize return travel.

---

Yes.

International patients may begin with an online consultation.

Preliminary planning may use:

- photographs
- medical history
- information about previous procedures

A remote assessment does not replace:

- physical examination

- final surgical evaluation required before the procedure

2. Do not imply that liposuction treats obesity.

3. Do not imply that removing more fat automatically creates a better result.

4. Do not imply that liposuction automatically tightens loose skin.

5. Treat localized fat and skin laxity as separate anatomical concerns.

6. Do not automatically recommend tummy tuck when abdominal fat is present.

7. Do not automatically recommend liposuction when loose abdominal skin is the primary problem.

8. Do not automatically recommend arm lift for every arm-contouring patient.

9. Do not automatically recommend thigh lift for every thigh-contouring patient.

10. Do not assume that every 360° liposuction patient requires a tummy tuck.

11. Do not imply that every combined procedure is appropriate for every patient.

12. Combined surgery requires individual assessment.

13. Clearly define 360° abdomen liposuction as:
    - front abdomen
    - waist
    - lower back

14. Do not describe 360° abdomen liposuction as treatment of only the front abdomen.

15. Do not guarantee a particular waist size.

16. Do not guarantee a specific body shape.

17. Do not guarantee an exact visual outcome.

18. Do not guarantee perfect symmetry.

19. Clearly state that results vary between patients.

20. Clearly state that future weight changes can affect results.

21. Do not present a universal recovery period.

22. Recovery should be linked to:
    - treatment area
    - surgical extent
    - number of areas
    - whether skin-removal surgery is included

23. Do not prescribe a fixed international stay for every patient.

24. Do not tell international patients to book flights based on a generic recovery schedule.

25. Online consultation does not replace:
    - physical examination
    - preoperative testing
    - final surgical assessment

26. Clearly label all published prices as:
    - starting prices
    - indicative prices
    - reference prices

27. Do not present the AUD figures as guaranteed final exchange-rate values.

28. Do not invent combined-package pricing.

29. Do not add individual prices together and call the result an official combined surgical package.

30. Do not invent pricing not present in the source.

31. Surgery should be described as being performed at City International Hospital.

32. Do not describe DR. MARIS AESTHETICS as the owner of City International Hospital.

33. Preserve direct surgeon involvement as a primary differentiator.

34. Do not create a high-volume or package-sales tone.

35. Maintain tone:
    - premium
    - medical
    - factual
    - individualized
    - calm
    - internationally oriented
    - transparent

36. Avoid:
    - aggressive transformation claims
    - "perfect body" claims
    - unrealistic before/after promises
    - guaranteed weight reduction
    - guaranteed waist measurements
    - fear-based messaging
    - invented clinical claims
    - invented recovery schedules
    - invented prices
    - aggressive discount language

## Preserve Decision Logic

Use this general logic:

### Localized Fat + Suitable Skin

Consider liposuction assessment.

### Significant Loose Skin

Evaluate skin-removal surgery.

### Fat + Loose Skin

Evaluate whether a combined strategy may be appropriate.

### Abdominal Wall Concern

Evaluate whether abdominoplasty / muscle repair is relevant.

---

## Do Not Turn Decision Logic into Automatic Diagnosis

The website should educate the patient but must not automatically decide which operation the patient needs.

Final determination requires assessment by Dr. Maris.

# CORE PAGE MESSAGE

Body contouring should begin by identifying what is actually causing the contour concern.

Localized fat, loose skin, tissue laxity and abdominal wall changes are different anatomical problems and may require different surgical approaches.

Liposuction primarily addresses localized fat.

Procedures such as tummy tuck, arm lift and thigh lift address significant loose or redundant skin, with abdominal surgery potentially also addressing abdominal wall concerns when clinically appropriate.

For some patients, a combined approach may be considered.

Dr. Maris personally assesses fat distribution, skin quality, previous surgery, body proportions and medical suitability before recommending a surgical plan.

The objective is not to perform the maximum number of procedures.

The objective is to select a surgical approach appropriate to the patient's anatomy, priorities and medical assessment.
