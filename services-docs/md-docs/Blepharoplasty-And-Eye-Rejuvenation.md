# SECTION 02 — UNDERSTANDING BLEPHAROPLASTY

## Component Type

Educational / procedure overview section

## Heading

Understanding Blepharoplasty

## Content

Blepharoplasty is plastic surgery performed on:

* upper eyelids
* lower eyelids
* or both

Ageing can affect several structures around the eyes.

Changes may include:

* looser skin
* changes in supporting tissues
* more noticeable fat above or below the eyes

Blepharoplasty may address selected concerns including:

* sagging upper eyelid skin
* lower eyelid excess skin
* bags beneath the eyes

In some patients, severe upper eyelid skin may also interfere with peripheral vision.

## Eye Rejuvenation Assessment

Cosmetic eye rejuvenation requires more than deciding that the eye area appears "tired."

Assessment should determine whether the concern relates primarily to:

* excess upper eyelid skin
* upper eyelid fat
* eyelid asymmetry
* lower eyelid bags
* loose lower eyelid skin
* eyebrow descent
* a combination of factors
* previous eyelid surgery

## Core Message

The correct operation depends on the anatomy creating the concern.

# SECTION 04 — WHO MAY CONSIDER UPPER EYELID SURGERY?

## Component Type

Candidate / indications section

## Heading

Who May Consider Upper Eyelid Surgery?

## Possible Concerns

Upper blepharoplasty may be considered when a patient is concerned about:

* loose or redundant upper eyelid skin
* heaviness around the upper eyelid
* eyelid crease obscured by excess tissue
* asymmetry between the upper eyelids
* changes in eyelid shape associated with ageing
* previous eyelid surgery requiring reassessment

## Functional Concerns

In more significant cases, upper eyelid skin can contribute to reduced peripheral vision.

This should be regarded as a functional concern and may require appropriate eye assessment rather than being treated as a purely cosmetic issue.

## Important Anatomical Rule

Upper eyelid surgery should not automatically be used to treat a low eyebrow.

If eyebrow descent contributes substantially to upper-eye heaviness, removing additional eyelid skin without recognizing brow position may not address the underlying problem.

# SECTION 05 — LOWER BLEPHAROPLASTY

## Component Type

Primary procedure detail section

## Heading

Lower Blepharoplasty

## Content

Lower blepharoplasty addresses selected ageing or contour concerns beneath the eyes.

## Common Reasons for Consultation

* prominent under-eye bags
* visible lower-eyelid fat
* loose lower-eyelid skin
* contour irregularity beneath the eyes
* asymmetry
* changes following previous lower-eyelid surgery

## General Surgical Information

Lower blepharoplasty may use different surgical approaches depending on anatomy.

General medical guidance describes approaches involving:

* an incision beneath the eyelashes
* or an incision inside the lower eyelid

Fat may potentially be:

* removed
* redistributed

depending on the surgical plan.

## Core Principle

Different lower-eyelid problems require different approaches.

A younger patient with prominent fat but minimal loose skin may require a different operation from a patient with substantial skin laxity.

## Website Rule

Do not advertise one universal lower-eyelid technique for every patient.

# SECTION 06 — UNDER-EYE BAGS ARE NOT ALWAYS THE SAME PROBLEM

## Component Type

High-value educational / anatomical diagnosis section

## Heading

Under-Eye Bags Are Not Always the Same Problem

## Content

Patients often describe all lower-eyelid fullness as "eye bags."

However, several anatomical factors can influence the appearance of the under-eye region.

These may include:

* prominent orbital fat
* loose skin
* lower-eyelid laxity
* differences in cheek volume
* fluid retention
* facial anatomy
* asymmetry

## Important Principle

Not every under-eye concern should automatically be treated by removing fat.

## Core Message

The procedure must be tailored to individual anatomy.

Photographs alone should not be used to promise a specific surgical result.

# SECTION 07 — EYEBROW LIFT

## Component Type

Related upper-face procedure section

## Heading

Eyebrow Lift

## Content

Some patients believe they need upper eyelid surgery when part of the heaviness around the eye is caused by a descending eyebrow.

An eyebrow lift is intended to reposition eyebrow tissue rather than primarily remove upper eyelid skin.

## Anatomical Relationship

The eyebrow and upper eyelid affect one another both:

* visually
* anatomically

For this reason, eyebrow position should be evaluated whenever upper-eyelid heaviness is present.

## Core Message

A low brow and excess eyelid skin are not the same anatomical problem.

Risks & Considerations

## Intro

Blepharoplasty is surgery and carries potential complications.

## Recognized Eyelid-Surgery Risks

* anesthesia complications
* bleeding
* changes in sensation
* difficulty closing the eyes
* dry eyes
* infection
* lower-eyelid ectropion
* temporary lower-eyelid pulling
* lid lag
* scarring
* visual changes
* need for revision surgery

## Rare but Serious Risks

Rare serious complications can occur.

General medical guidance includes possible:

* injury to eye muscles
* very rare loss of eyesight

These risks should not be hidden simply because blepharoplasty is commonly performed for cosmetic reasons.

Eyebrow lifting has a different risk profile.

* bleeding

* asymmetry

* visible scarring
* need for revision

Individual risks must be discussed before informed consent.

### FAQ 2 — What is the difference between upper and lower blepharoplasty?

Upper blepharoplasty addresses selected excess tissue of the upper eyelid.

Lower blepharoplasty addresses concerns beneath the eye such as:

* prominent fat bags
* loose lower-eyelid skin
* other selected contour concerns

The procedures involve different anatomy and should not automatically be combined.

---

### FAQ 3 — Do I need upper blepharoplasty or an eyebrow lift?

It depends on where the heaviness originates.

Excess upper-eyelid skin may be addressed with upper blepharoplasty.

A low eyebrow may require assessment of the brow itself.

Some patients have both concerns.

Evaluating the eyebrow before removing upper-eyelid skin helps avoid selecting a procedure based solely on appearance in photographs.

---

### FAQ 4 — Can upper and lower blepharoplasty be performed together?

They can potentially be combined in appropriately assessed patients.

Suitability depends on:

* eyelid anatomy
* health
* eye history
* extent of surgery required

The current DR. MARIS price document lists upper and lower blepharoplasty separately and does not provide an approved combined package price.

---

### FAQ 5 — Can lower blepharoplasty remove under-eye bags?

Lower blepharoplasty may improve under-eye bags when prominent fat or related lower-eyelid anatomy contributes to the concern.

Depending on the individual case, surgery may involve:

* removal of selected fat
* redistribution of selected fat
* management of other lower-eyelid tissues

Not every under-eye concern is caused by the same anatomical structure.

Assessment is therefore important.

---

### FAQ 6 — Will blepharoplasty leave scars?

Blepharoplasty requires surgical incisions.

It should not be described as scarless.

Upper-eyelid incisions are commonly positioned within the eyelid fold in general surgical practice.

Lower-eyelid surgery may use:

* an incision beneath the lashes
* or an incision inside the eyelid

depending on technique.

Scar visibility varies between patients and changes during healing.

---

### FAQ 7 — How long does swelling last after eyelid surgery?

Swelling and bruising generally improve progressively rather than disappearing immediately.

General medical guidance notes that much visible bruising and swelling often decreases over approximately:

**10–14 days**

Scars and subtle tissue changes continue settling for longer.

Your actual recovery depends on:

* operation performed
* extent of surgery
* individual healing

---

### FAQ 8 — Can eyelid surgery cause dry eyes?

Yes.

Temporary or persistent eye dryness is a recognized risk of blepharoplasty.

Other potential concerns include:

* difficulty closing the eyes
* irritation
* eyelid-position changes

Patients should tell Dr. Maris about:

* existing dry-eye symptoms
* eye medications
* previous ophthalmic conditions

before surgery.

---

### FAQ 9 — How long should I stay in Vietnam after having blepharoplasty?

The recommended stay depends on:

* upper-eyelid surgery
* lower-eyelid surgery
* combined procedures
* eyebrow lift
* wound healing
* postoperative review requirements

Dr. Maris should confirm the appropriate stay before a fixed return flight is booked.
