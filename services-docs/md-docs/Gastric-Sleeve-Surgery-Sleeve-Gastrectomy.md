# SECTION 02 — UNDERSTANDING GASTRIC SLEEVE SURGERY

## Component Type

Procedure education section

## Heading

Understanding Gastric Sleeve Surgery

## Content

Gastric sleeve surgery is the commonly used name for:

**Sleeve Gastrectomy**

also called:

**Vertical Sleeve Gastrectomy**

During the operation, a substantial portion of the stomach is surgically removed, leaving a narrower tube-shaped stomach.

## How It Works

The procedure:

* reduces stomach capacity
* alters appetite-related hormonal pathways
* affects metabolic signaling

Unlike gastric bypass, standard sleeve gastrectomy does not normally reroute the small intestine.

## Important Distinction

Gastric sleeve surgery is not a procedure designed simply to make the body appear slimmer.

It is a form of:

**metabolic and bariatric surgery**

used in appropriately selected patients as part of the medical management of:

* obesity
* obesity-related disease
* metabolic health conditions

# SECTION 04 — WHO MAY CONSIDER BARIATRIC SURGERY?

## Component Type

Candidate / eligibility section

## Heading

Who May Consider Bariatric Surgery?

## Important Principle

Eligibility should not be determined from:

* photographs
* body weight alone
* target weight alone
* appearance
* online BMI calculator alone

## Bariatric Assessment May Consider

* BMI
* obesity-related medical conditions
* metabolic disease
* previous attempts at non-surgical weight management
* overall surgical risk
* nutritional health
* psychological readiness
* ability to participate in long-term follow-up

## Guideline Context

The 2022 ASMBS/IFSO guidelines recommend metabolic and bariatric surgery for individuals with BMI of approximately:

**35 kg/m² or above**

regardless of the presence or severity of associated conditions.

Surgery may also be considered for selected patients with:

**BMI 30–34.9 kg/m²**

when metabolic disease is present.

The guidelines also recognize that lower BMI thresholds may apply to Asian populations.

## Critical Rule

These are professional guidelines.

They are not an automatic indication for surgery.

## Final Eligibility

The final decision should be made by an appropriately qualified bariatric team after clinical assessment.

# SECTION 07 — HOW SLEEVE GASTRECTOMY IS PERFORMED

## Component Type

Procedure explanation section

## Heading

How Sleeve Gastrectomy Is Performed

## General Medical Description

Sleeve gastrectomy is generally performed:

* in a hospital
* under general anesthesia

The operation is commonly performed laparoscopically using several small abdominal incisions.

## During Surgery

The stomach is divided vertically.

A substantial portion is removed.

The remaining stomach forms a narrower sleeve-shaped structure.

## Intestinal Anatomy

The intestines are not normally rerouted as part of a standard sleeve gastrectomy.

## Verification Required

The exact:

* surgical technique
* stapling system
* anesthesia protocol
* operative pathway
* leak-testing protocol
* perioperative pathway

must be determined by the treating bariatric surgeon.

## Website Rule

Do not describe a specific technique as the DR. MARIS technique unless verified.

Gastric Sleeve and Acid Reflux

## Content

Existing reflux symptoms should be discussed before choosing a bariatric procedure.

## Why This Matters

For some patients, pre-existing gastrointestinal conditions may influence whether:

* sleeve gastrectomy
* another bariatric procedure
* further gastrointestinal investigation

is appropriate.

## Core Message

Patients should not select gastric sleeve solely because:

* it is well known
* it appears simpler
* another person had the procedure
* it is advertised as the most popular option

Procedure selection should be made by a bariatric surgeon after medical assessment.

# SECTION 18 — FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

## Component Type

FAQ accordion

## Heading

Frequently Asked Questions

---

### FAQ 1 — What is gastric sleeve surgery?

Gastric sleeve surgery, or sleeve gastrectomy, is metabolic and bariatric surgery in which most of the stomach is surgically removed, leaving a narrower sleeve-shaped stomach.

The procedure:

* reduces stomach capacity
* affects appetite-related hormonal signaling

Unlike gastric bypass, a standard sleeve procedure does not normally reroute the intestines.

---

### FAQ 2 — Who qualifies for gastric sleeve surgery?

Eligibility depends on factors including:

* BMI
* metabolic health
* obesity-related conditions
* previous non-surgical weight-management attempts
* nutritional status
* psychological readiness
* surgical risk
* ability to participate in long-term follow-up

Updated ASMBS/IFSO guidance recommends metabolic and bariatric surgery for BMI of approximately 35 kg/m² or above and considers surgery at lower BMI levels in selected patients with metabolic disease.

Different thresholds may apply to Asian populations.

A bariatric surgeon must determine individual suitability.

---

### FAQ 3 — How much does gastric sleeve surgery cost with DR. MARIS?

An approved DR. MARIS gastric-sleeve price has not been supplied.

The current International Price List does not contain a bariatric surgery line item.

A price should not be estimated from competitor websites or other DR. MARIS procedures.

The page should only be updated when an approved DR. MARIS or partner-provider quotation is available.

---

### FAQ 4 — Is gastric sleeve surgery cosmetic surgery?

No.

Sleeve gastrectomy is metabolic and bariatric surgery used in the medical management of obesity.

It is fundamentally different from procedures such as:

* liposuction
* tummy tuck
* body contouring

The operation changes the stomach and requires:

* medical eligibility assessment
* hospital surgery
* nutritional care
* long-term follow-up

Cosmetic body-contouring surgery may be considered separately after significant weight loss in selected patients.

---

### FAQ 5 — Is the stomach reduction permanent?

The part of the stomach removed during sleeve gastrectomy is not simply restored after recovery.

The anatomical procedure is therefore generally considered irreversible.

However, this does not mean that:

* weight loss is guaranteed
* weight cannot be regained
* long-term follow-up is unnecessary

Weight regain can occur.

Patients require ongoing:

* dietary follow-up
* medical follow-up
* lifestyle participation

---

### FAQ 6 — What are the main risks of sleeve gastrectomy?

Potential early complications include:

* bleeding
* infection
* anesthesia complications
* blood clots
* respiratory problems
* gastric staple-line leakage

Longer-term concerns can include:

* reflux
* nutritional deficiencies
* vomiting
* obstruction
* inadequate weight loss
* weight regain

Individual risks depend on medical history and should be discussed with the bariatric surgeon.

---

### FAQ 7 — Can gastric sleeve surgery improve diabetes?

Metabolic and bariatric surgery can improve metabolic disease in appropriately selected patients, including some patients with type 2 diabetes.

However, improvement or remission should not be guaranteed.

Diabetes:

* medication
* glucose monitoring
* follow-up

should continue to be managed by the appropriate medical team.

---

### FAQ 8 — Will I need vitamins after gastric sleeve surgery?

Nutritional monitoring is an important part of long-term bariatric care.

Vitamin or mineral supplementation may be required.

The exact supplementation plan should be individualized according to:

* bariatric protocol
* dietary intake
* laboratory results
* nutritional assessment

Do not self-prescribe supplementation based solely on website information.

---

### FAQ 9 — Can I have a video consultation before flying to Vietnam?

Yes, a remote consultation may be useful for:

* preliminary assessment
* medical-history review
* identifying required investigations
* determining whether further bariatric assessment is appropriate

However, it cannot replace:

* physical examination
* anesthesia evaluation
* required preoperative testing
* final bariatric assessment

International patients should also establish a follow-up plan for after they return home.

---

### FAQ 10 — How long should I stay in Vietnam after gastric sleeve surgery?

A fixed duration cannot be responsibly published until the actual bariatric pathway is confirmed.

Stay length should account for:

* hospital recovery
* postoperative dietary progression
* hydration
* early complications
* postoperative review
* suitability for long-distance travel

The treating bariatric surgeon should clear the patient for travel rather than relying on a generic medical-tourism timetable.

# CORE PAGE MESSAGE

Sleeve gastrectomy is major metabolic and bariatric surgery used in the medical management of obesity in appropriately selected patients.

The procedure removes a substantial portion of the stomach, leaving a narrower sleeve-shaped stomach and affecting both stomach capacity and appetite-related metabolic pathways.

It is fundamentally different from liposuction and cosmetic body-contouring surgery.

Appropriate bariatric care begins with assessment of:

* BMI
* obesity-related disease
* metabolic health
* previous weight-management treatment
* nutritional health
* gastrointestinal symptoms
* psychological readiness
* surgical risk
* ability to participate in long-term follow-up

The operation itself is only one part of treatment.

Long-term:

* nutritional care
* medical monitoring
* dietary participation
* metabolic follow-up

remain important after surgery.

Weight loss, metabolic improvement and long-term outcomes vary between patients and should not be guaranteed.

For international patients, planning must also include a clear postoperative and long-term follow-up pathway after returning home.

The objective is not to offer the fastest possible route to weight loss.

The objective is to determine whether metabolic surgery is medically appropriate and whether the patient can safely participate in the full long-term bariatric care pathway.
