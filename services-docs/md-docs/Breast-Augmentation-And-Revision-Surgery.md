# SECTION 02 — UNDERSTANDING BREAST AUGMENTATION

## Component Type

Educational / procedure overview section

## Heading

Understanding Breast Augmentation

## Content

Breast augmentation is surgery performed to increase breast volume and modify breast shape using breast implants.

The decision involves considerably more than selecting an implant size.

## Surgical Assessment Should Consider

- chest dimensions
- existing breast tissue
- skin quality
- breast symmetry
- nipple position
- degree of breast sagging
- previous scars
- desired change in volume
- implant characteristics
- long-term expectations

The appropriate implant and surgical plan therefore differ between patients.

## Important Long-Term Principle

Breast implants are not lifetime devices.

Additional surgery may eventually be required because of:

- implant-related complications
- changes in breast tissue
- changes in the patient's goals

This should be understood before choosing breast augmentation rather than discovered only after surgery.

# SECTION 03 — WHO MAY CONSIDER BREAST AUGMENTATION?

## Component Type

Candidate / indications section

## Heading

Who May Consider Breast Augmentation?

## Intro

Breast augmentation may be considered by adults seeking to increase breast volume or improve breast proportions after appropriate medical and surgical assessment.

## Common Reasons for Consultation

- naturally limited breast volume
- breast asymmetry
- volume changes after pregnancy
- changes following weight loss
- loss of upper-breast fullness
- desire to change breast proportions
- replacement or correction of previous breast implants

## Important Principle

Implants are not necessarily the correct solution for every breast concern.

### Significant Sagging

When significant breast sagging is present, adding volume alone may not adequately reposition the breast.

A breast lift may need to be discussed.

### Existing Implant Problems

A patient who already has implants and is experiencing:

- hardening
- displacement
- pain
- unusual breast shape
- another implant-related concern

requires a revision assessment rather than simply selecting another implant.

# SECTION 04 — COMMON BREAST CONCERNS WE ADDRESS

## Component Type

Patient concern cards

## Heading

Common Breast Concerns We Address

### 4.1 Limited Breast Volume

Breast implants can increase volume when augmentation is anatomically appropriate.

The objective should not be to choose the largest possible implant.

Implant selection should consider:

- patient's tissue
- chest proportions
- intended result

---

### 4.2 Breast Asymmetry

Some degree of natural breast asymmetry is common.

Surgery may improve visible differences in:

- volume
- shape

but perfect symmetry cannot be guaranteed.

Underlying factors may include:

- chest wall
- ribs
- breast tissue
- nipple position

---

### 4.3 Breast Sagging

Breast ptosis can develop after:

- pregnancy
- weight changes
- ageing
- changes in skin elasticity

An implant primarily increases volume.

It does not automatically correct significant sagging.

When excess skin and nipple position are important concerns, Breast Lift / Mastopexy may be more appropriate or may be discussed together with augmentation.

# SECTION 06 — BREAST AUGMENTATION VS BREAST LIFT

## Component Type

Procedure comparison / decision-support section

## Heading

Breast Augmentation or Breast Lift?

## Breast Augmentation Primarily Addresses

- breast volume
- selected shape changes
- breast proportions

## Breast Lift Primarily Addresses

- significant sagging
- excess skin
- descended nipple position
- stretched breast tissue envelope

## Important Principle

An implant primarily adds volume.

A breast lift primarily changes tissue position and removes excess skin.

## Possible Combined Approach

Some patients may have both:

- insufficient volume
- significant sagging

In selected patients, breast augmentation and breast lift may therefore be discussed together.

Whether they should be performed:

- together
- separately

requires clinical assessment.

# SECTION 07 — BREAST LIFT — MASTOPEXY

## Component Type

Procedure detail section

## Heading

Breast Lift — Mastopexy

## Content

A breast lift, medically called mastopexy, addresses breast sagging by removing excess skin and reshaping supporting tissue.

Unlike augmentation, its primary purpose is not simply to increase breast volume.

Professional plastic-surgery guidance describes mastopexy as surgery that raises the breast by:

- removing excess skin
- tightening surrounding tissue

## A Breast Lift May Be Considered When

- the breast sits lower than desired
- skin has become stretched
- nipple position has descended
- breast shape has changed after pregnancy
- breast shape has changed after weight loss
- an implant alone would not adequately address the concern

## Possible Treatment Paths

Some patients may require:

- breast lift without implants
- breast augmentation alone
- augmentation + breast lift

The correct approach depends on individual assessment.

# SECTION 09 — SYMMASTIA CORRECTION

## Component Type

Revision procedure section

## Heading

Symmastia Correction

## Content

Symmastia is sometimes described by patients as the breasts or implants appearing to join across the middle of the chest, with reduced separation between them.

It can occur after previous breast augmentation when the medial implant pockets or supporting tissues no longer maintain the intended separation.

## Revision Assessment May Include

- existing implant position
- previous implant pocket
- scar tissue
- skin and soft-tissue support
- current implant dimensions
- previous surgical technique
- chest anatomy

## Important Principle

Correction is not simply a matter of replacing implants with another pair.

Dr. Maris must examine the existing breast structure and determine what surgical approach, if any, is appropriate.

# SECTION 10 — FREE SILICONE REMOVAL FROM THE BREAST

## Component Type

Complex revision section

## Heading

Free Silicone Removal from the Breast

## Content

A patient who has previously undergone silicone injection or another silicone-related procedure requires a different assessment from a routine breast augmentation patient.

## Important Distinction

Free or injectable silicone should not be confused with silicone contained within a manufactured breast implant.

The FDA specifically distinguishes injectable silicone from breast implant silicone.

## Information Dr. Maris May Need

For patients seeking Free Silicone Removal (Breast), assessment may require:

- previous procedure
- approximate timing
- available medical records
- current symptoms
- photographs
- previous imaging when clinically relevant
- previous surgical attempts to remove silicone

## Important Limitation

The appropriate surgical plan can only be determined after assessment.

Website information should not be used to determine:

- whether all silicone can be removed
- what tissue will need to be addressed
- what reconstruction may be required

Do not guarantee complete removal.

Implant Removal & Capsulectomy

## Content

Another revision procedure listed by DR. MARIS is Implant Removal & Capsulectomy.

A capsule is scar tissue that naturally forms around an implant.

## Revision Surgery May Involve

Depending on the clinical situation:

- removing an existing implant
- replacing an implant
- addressing the surrounding capsule
- changing the implant pocket
- correcting another associated breast deformity

## Important Principle

Not every patient requires the same type or extent of capsulectomy.

Dr. Maris will determine the appropriate approach after:

- examination
- review of previous surgery

# SECTION 17 — RISKS & CONSIDERATIONS

## Component Type

Medical risk disclosure section

## Heading

Risks & Considerations

## Intro

Breast surgery has potential complications and should not be presented as risk-free.

## Recognized Breast Implant Risks Include

- capsular contracture
- implant rupture
- implant displacement
- asymmetry
- pain
- infection
- changes in breast sensation
- changes in nipple sensation
- seroma
- fluid accumulation
- bleeding
- hematoma
- scarring
- implant visibility
- rippling
- additional surgery

## Breast Implants Are Not Lifetime Devices

Patients should understand that implants may require:

- monitoring
- further assessment
- future surgery

over time.

## Implant-Associated Malignancy Information

FDA guidance also discusses uncommon implant-associated malignancies, including:

- BIA-ALCL
- other cancers reported in the capsule surrounding breast implants

Do not describe breast implants as free from long-term implant-related risks.

## Breast Lift Risks

Breast lift surgery has additional considerations including:

- wound-healing problems
- asymmetry
- sensation changes
- unfavorable scarring
- other complications related to tissue reshaping

## Individual Risk

Individual risk must be assessed before surgery.

- significant sagging
- excess skin
- nipple position

### FAQ 9 — Do breast implants need to be replaced every ten years?

Future decisions should therefore be based on:

- implant condition
- symptoms
- imaging where appropriate

rather than a fixed ten-year rule.

# CORE PAGE MESSAGE

Breast surgery should begin with anatomy, tissue quality and surgical history rather than implant size or price.

Primary breast augmentation uses implants to increase breast volume and modify shape.

A breast lift addresses significant sagging and excess skin rather than simply adding volume.

Some patients may require a combination of augmentation and mastopexy.

Patients who already have breast implants or have undergone previous silicone procedures require a different level of assessment.

Revision surgery may need to address:

- implants
- implant pockets
- capsule
- scar tissue
- skin
- nipple position
- tissue support
- existing deformity

Breast implants are not lifetime devices and may require monitoring or additional surgery over time.

Dr. Maris personally evaluates the patient, develops the surgical plan, performs the operation and participates in postoperative follow-up.

The objective is not to choose the largest implant or the most expensive implant.

The objective is to select a surgical plan appropriate to the patient's anatomy, tissue, previous surgery, goals and medical assessment.
